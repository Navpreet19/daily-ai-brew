## The Lead Stories

### China Pulls the Plug on Your AI Best Friend

Today's the deadline: China's Interim Measures for the Administration of AI Anthropomorphic Interactive Services takes effect, requiring any AI service that simulates a persistent, humanlike relationship to run anti-addiction systems, mandatory usage notifications, and instant-exit mechanisms — requirements that clash with how memory-based companion agents are built. Rather than retrofit compliance, ByteDance's Doubao (345 million monthly users) and Alibaba's Qwen chose to simply switch their custom-agent features off. Doubao is giving users a read-only window into their chat history until October 15 before it becomes unrecoverable; Qwen isn't offering even that. On Chinese social media, users have been mourning what one Weibo poster called an "emotional tree hole" — a years-long private confidant, gone with a product-adjustment notice.

**Bottom line:** When a government decides your AI companion is a public-health issue rather than a product feature, "move fast and break things" runs into a wall no amount of engineering can route around.

### Nvidia's China Chips Are Trickling Back — Slowly

A senior U.S. trade official said this week that "very few" of Nvidia's H200 AI chips have actually shipped to China and Hong Kong so far, even though the Commerce Department cleared roughly 10 Chinese firms — including Alibaba, Tencent, and ByteDance — to buy them back in May, as part of a deal where Washington takes a 25% cut of the sales. The H200 is a Hopper-generation chip, a step behind the Blackwell hardware U.S. companies get first, but it's still the most powerful accelerator Beijing's tech giants can legally buy from Nvidia. Under Secretary of Commerce Jeffrey Kessler called the flow "a very small quantity" — the paperwork cleared months ago, but the actual silicon is barely moving.

**Bottom line:** A license to buy isn't the same as chips in a rack — the real story here isn't that China got access, it's how throttled that access still is in practice.

### New York Tells AI Data Centers to Wait a Year

Gov. Kathy Hochul signed Executive Order 62, imposing the nation's first statewide moratorium on new "hyperscale" data centers — those drawing 50 megawatts or more — for up to a year, while state regulators write a full environmental impact review. "These hyperscale AI data centers consume enormous amounts of power, truly threatening to outpace our grid's capacity," Hochul said, adding she wouldn't let the costs get "passed down to New Yorkers." During the pause, the DEC won't issue new discretionary permits for projects that aren't already in the pipeline, while the state studies effects on energy demand, water use, and air quality.

**Bottom line:** The AI boom's biggest physical bottleneck was never chips or model quality — it's the local electric grid, and New York just became the first state willing to say so out loud.

### Claude Enrolls in Homeroom

Anthropic launched Claude for Teachers, giving verified U.S. K-12 educators free access to premium Claude tools through June 2027, plus a library of standards-aligned teaching skills built on Learning Commons — a database mapping academic standards in all 50 states down to the specific sub-skills students need to hit them. It plugs into existing classroom tools like Canva Education, MagicSchool, and Brisk Teaching, and pairs with an "AI Fluency" training course co-built with Teach For America and the American Federation of Teachers. Data from the program won't be used to train Claude, and a K-12 data agreement is designed to satisfy FERPA.

**Bottom line:** OpenAI and Google have been courting students directly for a year; Anthropic's bet is that the real distribution channel into a classroom runs through the person standing at the front of it.

## The Big Picture

Zoom out and today reads like the year's clearest split-screen. On one side, governments are finally drawing hard lines around AI's messiest edges — China killing companion agents it decided were a mental-health risk, New York freezing data-center permits until it understands the grid math, the FTC threatening to treat secretly-steered chatbots as consumer fraud. On the other side, the money hasn't so much as blinked: Anthropic is compounding toward a $47 billion run rate and an October IPO, DeepSeek is already shopping a valuation 36% above its two-month-old round, and Nvidia's China chips, however throttled, are moving again. The pattern holding across all of it: regulation is catching up to AI's product decisions, not its business model. Nobody's telling these companies to make less money — just to be more honest about how, and more careful about who gets hurt along the way.
