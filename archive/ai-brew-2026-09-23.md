## The Lead Stories

### The War Room's Newest Guest List

Wednesday brought the UN Security Council's first-ever high-level briefing dedicated to AI safety, with French Foreign Minister Jean-Noël Barrot chairing a session that put OpenAI's Sam Altman, Anthropic's Dario Amodei, and Hugging Face's Clément Delangue in front of all 15 members. Yoshua Bengio, co-chair of the UN's independent scientific panel, told the Council that researchers have long warned three conditions could cause a loss of human control over AI — a misaligned goal, the capability to pursue it, and an environment that allows it — and pointed to this summer's Hugging Face breach as a real-world preview. Altman pushed for shared global benchmarks on AI capability and safeguards, and publicly backed Amodei's proposal for embedded, independent evaluators. China's DeepSeek and Moonshot were invited too; DeepSeek founder Liang Wenfeng reportedly skipped it.

**Bottom line:** getting the labs that build the risk into the room that handles the world's worst crises is progress of a kind — but a briefing isn't a resolution, and nothing said Wednesday required anyone to actually change course.

### Safety's on the Agenda. Chips Aren't.

Xi Jinping lands in Washington Thursday for his one-on-one with President Trump, with AI sharing top billing alongside trade, rare earths, and Taiwan — but officials say the specific fight over chip export controls is staying off the table, even as both sides flesh out the AI-incident hotline Treasury Secretary Scott Bessent and Vice Premier He Lifeng sketched out Sunday. Analysts aren't expecting a breakthrough, just a joint statement gesturing at safety cooperation without either side giving up real leverage. Nvidia has a lot riding on the outcome regardless: its own guidance assumes zero data-center chip sales to China this year, meaning any surprise concession Thursday could move the number investors care about most.

**Bottom line:** Washington and Beijing can agree to talk about AI safety all they want; the actual leverage — chips, rare earths, market access — is a separate negotiation neither side is ready to start.

### The Merger That Wasn't a Merger, Allegedly

The Justice Department is investigating whether Nvidia structured its $20 billion licensing deal with chip startup Groq to dodge the antitrust review a traditional acquisition would have triggered, Bloomberg reported. Under the December agreement, Nvidia took a non-exclusive license to Groq's chip technology and hired away its top executives — founder Jonathan Ross and COO Sunny Madra both now work at Nvidia — without technically buying the company, leaving Groq nominally independent. The DOJ opened its inquiry shortly after the deal was announced and has since sent Nvidia a formal request for information; investigators could seek fines, though unwinding the deal is considered unlikely. It's the latest sign regulators are scrutinizing "acqui-hire" structures across AI as top labs and chipmakers absorb smaller rivals' talent and technology without a merger filing.

**Bottom line:** Nvidia didn't buy Groq on paper, but it got the founder, the COO, and the technology anyway — and the DOJ wants to know if that's a distinction with or without a difference.

### The $3 Million Model With a Claude-Shaped Shadow

Xiaomi open-sourced MiMo-V2.6-Pro this week, and independent benchmarking firm Artificial Analysis now ranks it the world's top open-weights model — tied with xAI's Grok 4.7 and 10 points ahead of DeepSeek's V4.1 Pro — despite Xiaomi saying the entire training run cost roughly $3 million. The timing is awkward: Anthropic says it disrupted an industrial-scale "distillation" campaign in which Xiaomi allegedly replayed more than 400,000 real MiMo user conversations and coding sessions through Claude last spring, mining the outputs for training data. Xiaomi hasn't addressed the accusation publicly, and no direct evidence yet ties MiMo-V2.6 specifically to Claude-derived data — but Anthropic's new Opus 5.5, launched Tuesday, shipped with an anti-distillation safeguard built for exactly this scenario.

**Bottom line:** a $3 million open-weights model beating billion-dollar labs would be a great story on its own; it's a more complicated one when the billion-dollar lab says it caught you copying its homework first.

## The Big Picture

Zoom out and today is one story wearing four different jackets: everyone with power over AI is building machinery to watch everyone else's power over AI. The UN got labs from rival powers into one room; Washington and Beijing are building a hotline instead of a treaty; the DOJ is checking whether Nvidia's biggest deal dodged the review process built for exactly this kind of consolidation. And then there's Xiaomi, which appears to have gotten ahead of the pack the old-fashioned way — by allegedly copying off the model everyone else is busy trying to regulate. Every institution in this issue is racing to build guardrails for AI power that keeps finding new ways to move faster than the guardrails.
