Memory chips go public, AI models go home

$11B — SambaNova's new valuation after closing the first $1 billion of a Series F, with JPMorgan Chase as its newest inference customer.
$2B — MiniMax's fresh Hong Kong share-and-bond raise to keep feeding its AI infrastructure habit.
3 — the Chinese AI giants (Alibaba, ByteDance, Z.ai) reportedly in talks with Beijing about locking foreigners out of their best models.

Mood: nobody's renting today. Wall Street wants equity in the actual supply chain, and governments on both sides of the Pacific want a say in who gets to use what.

Happy Saturday. While you were sleeping in, SK Hynix quietly pulled off the biggest foreign IPO in U.S. history, OpenAI shipped an app that wants to run your entire workday, and Beijing started thinking about building a wall around its own AI models. Weekend or not, the AI news cycle doesn't believe in days off. Neither do we — let's get you caught up.

SK Hynix Cashes In Its Chips (Literally)

SK Hynix priced its U.S. share offering at $149 Friday, raising roughly $26.5 billion — the biggest first-time listing by a foreign company on a U.S. exchange ever, edging out Alibaba's $25 billion debut back in 2014. The stock, trading as American depositary receipts under "SKHYV," jumped 13% on day one to close around $168, with demand reportedly running about 7x the available shares. SK Hynix supplies roughly 60% of the world's high-bandwidth memory chips — the specialized stacks that feed Nvidia's AI accelerators — and says the fresh capital will fund new production facilities back home in Korea. Regular trading resumes Monday under the permanent ticker SKHY.

Bottom line: Investors who couldn't get direct exposure to Nvidia's supply chain just got their biggest opportunity yet, and they used it — memory chips are now a Wall Street darling in their own right.

OpenAI Finally Ships Its Everything App

OpenAI launched ChatGPT Work Thursday, a unified desktop app bundling chat, coding, web browsing, and computer-use automation — clicking, typing, running multi-step workflows — into one tool built on the new GPT-5.6 Sol model. Type "@" and it pulls live context from Slack, Salesforce, or Microsoft 365; a built-in Sites tool lets you prompt your way to a hosted website. Desktop access is free for everyone starting now, with web and mobile following for paid, Enterprise, and Edu tiers within roughly 24 hours, per OpenAI. It's a direct shot at Anthropic's Claude Cowork, which beat OpenAI to the "AI that just does your job" pitch earlier this year.

Bottom line: The chatbot wars are basically over. The "does your entire workflow autonomously on your actual computer" wars just started, and OpenAI made its opening move free.

Beijing May Take Its Best Models Back Home

China's Ministry of Commerce has spent the past month in talks with Alibaba, ByteDance, and startup Z.ai about restricting overseas access to the country's most advanced AI models, Reuters reported, potentially covering both closed systems and open-weight releases — including ones not yet public. Officials are also weighing whether leaking proprietary AI technology should become a national-security offense. The scope is still undecided, and sources say it's unclear whether any rules take effect at all, but the direction is clear: China is starting to treat frontier AI the way the U.S. treats advanced chips — a strategic asset to guard, not export. One reported trigger for the discussions: concern that Washington could deploy Chinese models against Chinese interests.

Bottom line: The AI cold war just got a new front. It's no longer only about who gets access to chips — it's about who gets access to the models themselves.

SambaNova Banks a Billion, Wins JPMorgan

SambaNova closed the first $1 billion tranche of a Series F round led by General Atlantic, valuing the AI-chip and infrastructure company at $11 billion — its second mega-round in five months. Backers include BlackRock, T. Rowe Price, Qatar Investment Authority, and Intel Capital. The bigger win might be the customer, not the check: JPMorgan Chase picked SambaNova as an inference partner, deploying its SN40 and SN50 systems to run secure, on-premises AI inference inside the bank rather than leaning on outside cloud providers. A second close is expected within weeks.

Bottom line: Nvidia still owns training, but banks and other regulated industries are shopping for inference they can run behind their own firewall — and SambaNova just landed the reference client every rival wants.

  • 🏛️ The FTC wants public comment by July 31 on a proposal that AI companies secretly steering their outputs toward undisclosed goals could count as deceptive practice under federal law.
The Big Picture

Every story today is really about controlling a choke point. Wall Street just got its biggest-ever direct stake in the physical supply chain behind AI, courtesy of SK Hynix. Capital keeps flooding into compute regardless of geography, whether that's SambaNova's inference chips or MiniMax's infrastructure binge. And on both sides of the Pacific, governments are tightening who gets through the gate — Beijing weighing export controls on its own frontier models, Washington's FTC policing what those models are even allowed to say. Meanwhile OpenAI's new "super app" is a bet that the real prize isn't the model at all, it's owning the desktop where the work actually happens. The frontier-model race made the headlines for two years; the fight over infrastructure, capital, and access is where the action's moved now.

One More Thing

MiniMax's CEO Yan Junjie is forgoing his own salary until the company reaches AGI, while handing 4% of the company's equity to employees over four years. Somewhere between "genuinely committed founder" and "very on-brand retention strategy," it's at least a more concrete AGI timeline bet than most VCs are willing to make out loud.
