## The Lead Stories

### Anthropic Turns a Profit, Then Goes Shopping

Anthropic told investors this week it's on track for its first-ever profitable quarter — $10.9 billion in Q2 revenue, more than double Q1's $4.8 billion, with a projected $559 million operating profit. Caveat: that figure excludes stock-based compensation and comes from a fundraising deck, not an audited filing, since Anthropic isn't yet a public company. Almost in the same breath, Anthropic is reportedly in talks to buy Decart AI — an Israeli startup founded by Unit 8200 veterans that builds software to squeeze more efficiency out of AI chips and generate real-time video — for roughly $6 billion, its largest acquisition to date. Decart has raised about $450 million and was valued at just $4 billion three months ago. Nothing's signed on either front, and Anthropic has warned investors it may not hold onto profitability once next-generation training costs ramp back up.

**Bottom line:** nothing says "IPO-ready" like posting your first profit and immediately spending $6 billion of it on a shopping spree — Anthropic did both in the same week.

### The Landlords Want a Stock Ticker Too

Vantage Data Centers — the hyperscale developer backed by Silver Lake and DigitalBridge — is exploring an IPO or outright sale that could value the company near $100 billion, sources say, which would make it the largest data-center listing ever. Vantage has raised roughly $11 billion since late 2023, including a $9.2 billion equity round from its backers, building the power-hungry real estate AI labs increasingly rent instead of own. Talks are still informal and no process has formally launched, but the timing tracks a summer of AI-infrastructure firms testing public markets: Intel just sold $15 billion in stock for the first time since its 1971 IPO, and rival operator Switch is reportedly lining up banks for an IPO of its own that could value it up to $80 billion.

**Bottom line:** when the companies that just build and lease the buildings are angling to be worth as much as the AI labs renting them, the real money in this boom might be in the plumbing, not the software.

### India Just Landed One of Its Biggest GPU Deals Yet

Gorilla Technology and Yotta Data Services signed a long-term deal to deploy roughly 640 Nvidia HGX B200 servers — north of 5,000 GPUs — at Yotta's Navi Mumbai data center, with Gorilla projecting over $500 million in revenue from the arrangement over five years. Yotta will run the hardware as hyperscale GPU clusters, bare-metal compute, and AI model endpoints for Indian enterprise and government clients; the deployment also counts toward Yotta's existing commitment of 9,216 GPUs to the government-backed IndiaAI Mission, part of a stated goal to scale past a million GPUs within three to five years. It's the latest sign India wants to build sovereign AI compute at home rather than lease all of it from US hyperscalers.

**Bottom line:** every GPU India racks up domestically is one less favor it has to ask Washington or Beijing for later — sovereign AI, it turns out, starts with sovereign silicon.

### The Job Market Is Already Pricing In the Robots

More than half — 54% — of this year's 322 mass-layoff events, covering upwards of 170,000 workers, have explicitly cited AI or automation as a driver, according to one running tracker, out of roughly 206,000 job cuts total in 2026 so far. Salesforce says it used AI agents to shrink its support staff from 9,000 to 5,000; Oracle's latest regulatory filing warns AI adoption "has resulted, and may continue to result, in reductions to our workforce"; Microsoft cut roughly 9,000 jobs at the start of its fiscal year citing cost control amid rising AI infrastructure spending. Researchers caution plenty of these cuts are speculative cost-cutting dressed up as AI strategy to please investors, not proof AI has actually replaced anyone yet.

**Bottom line:** whether or not AI is really doing the job, companies have learned that blaming it plays well with investors — and that framing alone can cost workers as much as the real thing would.

## The Big Picture

Zoom out and today's stories share one thread: everyone in AI is converting this year's momentum into something permanent before anyone asks for the receipts. Anthropic didn't just post a profit — it moved immediately to spend it on infrastructure that protects the next one. Data-center landlords that used to be happy collecting rent from AI labs now want the same public-market validation the labs get. India isn't waiting for Washington or Beijing to hand it compute — it's buying GPUs by the thousands so it never has to ask. And the labor market is already restructuring around AI's promise, layoff by layoff, whether or not the productivity gains are fully proven yet. None of that is bubble-bursting behavior; it's the opposite — an industry racing to lock in its gains while the numbers still look good.
