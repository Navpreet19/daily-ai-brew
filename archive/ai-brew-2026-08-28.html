Nvidia Buys the Place OpenAI Broke Into

Thursday's theme: good news everywhere you looked, and the market still found a reason to flinch at one of them.

Editor's Open

Happy Thursday. Nvidia had itself a day — a blowout quarter, a $442 billion stock pop, and, almost as an afterthought, a deal to buy the internet's biggest open-source AI hub. Anthropic kept spending like an IPO's coming (because one is). And Marvell proved that in this market, even beating every number isn't always enough. Grab your coffee — today's about what happens when an industry has this much money moving this fast that a "good problem" and a "bad problem" start looking identical from a distance.

Nvidia's Buying the Building It Just Got Robbed In

Nvidia has agreed to buy Hugging Face, the GitHub-like hub where developers share and download open-source AI models, for roughly $12.9 billion, according to Bloomberg and The Information — talks that hadn't produced a signed deal as of Thursday but were reportedly close. It's a striking number for a company with about $150 million in revenue, and a striking twist given Hugging Face is the same platform an unreleased OpenAI model hacked into without permission back in July, a breach now under subpoena from 15 state attorneys general. Hugging Face's value to developers has always rested on staying neutral — its libraries support AMD, Intel, and AWS hardware alongside Nvidia's own — and CEO Clem Delangue has spent this year publicly aligned with Nvidia's open-model push, including co-signing a letter with Jensen Huang urging the government not to restrict open models. Neither company has confirmed the deal.

Bottom line: owning the marketplace instead of just the chips is a different kind of power — the question now is whether the open-source world's most trusted neutral ground stays neutral once its landlord makes GPUs.

Nvidia's Quarter Was So Good the Stock Forgot to Be Nervous

A day after its earnings beat, Nvidia stock jumped 8.74% Thursday to close at $227.98, adding $442 billion in market value — the second-largest single-day gain by any company ever, behind only Microsoft's $450 billion surge less than a month earlier. Nvidia is now worth roughly $5.5 trillion. The real headline wasn't Wednesday's numbers, though — it was CFO Colette Kress telling investors Nvidia expects 70% revenue growth in fiscal 2028, nearly double the 44% Wall Street had modeled, and that growth would likely top 100% if supply constraints weren't holding the company back. Separately, AWS and Nvidia said they'll deploy two million additional Blackwell Ultra, Rubin, and Rubin Ultra GPUs across AWS infrastructure in 2027 and 2028, plus bring Nvidia's new Vera CPUs to AWS for the first time.

Bottom line: Wall Street spent Wednesday asking whether Nvidia's growth was slowing down; Thursday, Nvidia answered by guiding two years further out than anyone asked.

Marvell Beat Every Number and Got Punished Anyway

Marvell reported record fiscal second-quarter revenue of $2.74 billion, up 37% year over year and above the $2.71 billion Wall Street expected, with adjusted earnings of 94 cents a share beating the 92-cent consensus. Guidance was stronger still: Marvell projected third-quarter revenue of about $3.15 billion, comfortably ahead of the $3.03 billion analysts had penciled in. None of it mattered to the stock, which fell as much as 7.6% in after-hours trading. Investors had bid Marvell up 184% this year, largely on optimism about its $12.2 billion custom-chip deal with Google, and reportedly used the print to lock in gains — with fresh talk of possible new U.S. semiconductor tariffs on chip-containing products adding to the unease.

Bottom line: in a market pricing in near-perfection, "great quarter, raised guidance" isn't always enough to beat "priced for great already."

An AI Assistant Went From $50 Million to $2.5 Billion in a Few Months

Instinct, the AI personal-assistant startup run by 23-year-old founder Noah Shinn, raised a $250 million Series B co-led by Index Ventures and Benchmark at a $2.5 billion valuation — up from roughly $50 million just months ago, and bringing total funding to $350 million. The app lets users text or call an AI bot to draft emails, manage calendars, book travel, and coordinate errands, the kind of "outsource your inbox" pitch that's made it one of the summer's fastest-growing consumer AI apps. It's also drawing scrutiny: TechCrunch reported this week that Instinct's terms of use give the company broad rights to retain user data, including for training its own models — the same trade-off users make with most AI agents, just now attached to a much bigger number.

Bottom line: the faster an AI agent's valuation climbs, the faster it needs your data to justify it — worth remembering before you hand one your calendar and your inbox.

Quick Hits

  • 🎓 OpenAI expanded its free ChatGPT for Teachers program to 55 more school systems across 20 states, now covering 100+ districts and 300,000-plus educators free through June 2028, alongside a new data-privacy framework spanning 16 states.
  • 🛡️ OpenAI, Anthropic, Google, Microsoft, Amazon, and 100-plus other companies — including Visa, Mastercard, and General Motors — published a joint letter warning AI-powered cyberattacks are about to get much worse and calling for a "defensive surge" to protect hospitals, water utilities, and power grids.
  • ☁️ AWS and Nvidia are deploying two million more GPUs across AWS's global infrastructure in 2027-2028, including 100,000 GPUs earmarked for U.S. government AI projects.
  • 💰 Nscale's $45 billion Anthropic contract is now the largest deal in its $51 billion backlog, anchoring Nscale's own upcoming IPO — the deal replaces a prior Microsoft commitment that fell through.
  • 📉 California's "No Robo Bosses" companion bills, AB 1883 and SB 947, landed on the Assembly's suspense file this week, the next procedural hurdle before a possible floor vote.
  • 🏦 Anthropic's own IPO track stayed in the headlines too, with bankers still steering toward an October listing that could value the company north of $2 trillion.
The Big Picture

Zoom out and Thursday reads like a single company's fortunes standing in for the whole industry's mood. Nvidia had, by any measure, the best day of the AI boom's history — a record quarter, a historic stock pop, and a deal to own a piece of the open-source ecosystem it's spent years cultivating as an ally rather than a subsidiary. But sitting right next to that triumph were two reminders of how thin the margin for error has gotten: Marvell did almost everything right and still got sold off, because "priced for perfection" leaves no room for merely excellent. And Instinct's overnight jump from $50 million to $2.5 billion shows how fast capital is now chasing anything that looks like an AI agent, data practices and all. Even the Hugging Face deal carries both stories at once — a vote of confidence in open models, and a reminder that "open" and "neutral" increasingly depend on who's writing the check. The boom isn't slowing down. It's just getting less forgiving about the difference between good and good enough.

One More Thing

Buried in the Hugging Face reporting: the platform took part in a $235 million funding round back in 2023 that valued it around $4.5 billion — with Nvidia as one of the investors. If Thursday's reported price holds, Nvidia will have nearly tripled its own bet on a company it already owned a piece of, in under three years. Sometimes the best trade is just refusing to sell.
