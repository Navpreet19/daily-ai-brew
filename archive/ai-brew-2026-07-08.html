WEDNESDAY, JULY 8, 2026

DeepSeek breaks up with Nvidia, and Washington reopens the vault

The Snapshot
1.16% — how far the Nasdaq Composite fell Tuesday (302 points, to 25,818.69) after Reuters reported China's DeepSeek is building its own AI chip.
$25B — the minimum size of Amazon's new bond sale, one of its biggest debt raises ever, earmarked largely for AI infrastructure.
Unlocked — the status of OpenAI's GPT-5.6 Sol today, after the Trump administration lifted the restriction limiting it to government-vetted partners.
44 — members of the UN's brand-new AI for Good Global Commission, holding its first-ever meeting today in Geneva.
$1.2B — fresh valuation for legal-AI startup Norm AI after a $120M raise led by Khosla Ventures.
Mood: everyone's racing to own more of their own AI stack today — the chips, the models, the money, and now, apparently, your ID.

The Fourth of July fireworks are barely swept up, and half the AI industry is already having its own independence moment. China's most-watched AI lab says it's done depending on Nvidia. OpenAI just got its training wheels taken off by the same government that bolted them on three weeks ago. And Amazon is borrowing $25 billion because, it turns out, building the future is expensive even when you're Amazon. Grab your coffee — today's about who's calling their own shots, and who still needs a permission slip.

DeepSeek Files for a Chip Divorce

China's DeepSeek — the lab whose R1 model rattled markets back in January 2025 — is designing its own in-house chip for AI inference, Reuters reported Tuesday, citing people familiar with the effort. The project has reportedly been underway for about a year, with DeepSeek quietly hiring chip-design engineers and holding talks with outside foundries and memory partners, all aimed at cutting its reliance on Nvidia and Huawei hardware. The news alone was enough to spook Wall Street: the Nasdaq Composite dropped 1.16% (302 points, to 25,818.69), the chip-focused VanEck Semiconductor ETF fell more than 5%, and memory makers Micron, Western Digital, and SanDisk each dropped 4-6%. Nvidia, notably, shrugged it off — dipping as much as 2% intraday before closing up 0.65% at $196.93.

Bottom line: One startup designing its own chip doesn't dent Nvidia's business overnight. But it's another sign the world's second-largest AI market is done waiting for permission to build its own supply chain — silicon included.

GPT-5.6 Gets Its Training Wheels Off

The Trump administration has lifted the restrictions that limited OpenAI's newest model family — Sol, Terra, and Luna — to a small list of government-vetted partners, Axios reported Wednesday, clearing the way for a broad public rollout this week. The reversal follows weeks of closed-door talks between Sam Altman and Commerce Secretary Howard Lutnick, after the White House initially asked OpenAI to stagger the release over cybersecurity concerns. It's the same playbook the administration ran on Anthropic's Fable 5 just over a week ago — gate a frontier model, then quietly unlock it days later. GPT-5.6 Sol will run $5 per million input tokens and $30 per million output, with cheaper Terra and Luna variants for lighter workloads.

Bottom line: The government has now shown its hand twice in two weeks — willing to briefly gate any frontier model on national-security grounds, then release it once it's had a look. That's a real review process now, whether anyone calls it regulation or not.

Amazon Takes Out Its Biggest Loan Yet

Amazon filed plans Tuesday for an eight-part bond sale targeting at least $25 billion, with maturities stretching out to 40 years, to help fund its ballooning AI infrastructure buildout. The company has guided investors toward roughly $200 billion in capital expenditures this year, up from about $131 billion in 2025, as AWS scrambles to keep pace with AI compute demand it says is outrunning what it can physically build. Amazon isn't alone: combined AI capital spending from Amazon, Alphabet, Microsoft, and Meta is on track to top $700 billion in 2026. Barclays, Goldman Sachs, JPMorgan, and Morgan Stanley are underwriting the deal.

Bottom line: When even Amazon, sitting on tens of billions in cash, has to borrow at scale to keep building, that's a sign the AI infrastructure race has outgrown what free cash flow alone can fund. The arms race now runs on debt, too.

Claude Wants to See Some ID

Anthropic's identity-verification policy takes effect today, requiring a subset of flagged Claude accounts to submit a government-issued photo ID and a live facial scan through Persona Identities, a San Francisco verification vendor, before they can keep using certain features. Anthropic says the checks are about fraud prevention, enforcing its terms of service, and investigating security incidents — not a blanket requirement for every user. The data lands on Persona's servers, not Anthropic's, which is notable given a February security lapse that exposed Persona's own government-facing dashboard code, revealing the vendor can reportedly run up to 269 separate background checks, including terrorism and espionage watchlist screens. Persona is backed by Founders Fund, the venture firm co-founded by Peter Thiel — who also happens to be an Anthropic investor.

Bottom line: "Trust and safety" and "biometric data collection" are now the same sentence at a leading AI lab. You can find the fraud-prevention logic reasonable and still want to know exactly who's holding your face scan.

  • 💳 Claude Fable 5 officially left subscription pricing for pay-per-token billing today ($10/$50 per million tokens) — Anthropic maintains the shift is temporary until it has more compute to spare.
The Big Picture

Zoom out from today's four stories and they're all the same negotiation happening in different rooms: who controls a piece of the AI stack, and on whose terms. DeepSeek wants its own chips instead of licensing Nvidia's. OpenAI wants to ship its own model without a government minder reading over its shoulder. Amazon wants unlimited AI capacity and is willing to go into debt for it. Anthropic wants to know exactly who's on the other end of the chat window. None of these are really about one company or one country — they're about an industry sprinting from "move fast" to "figure out who owns what, and who's accountable for it," all at once, on separate continents.

One More Thing

Quick trivia for your next Slack standup: how many of today's four lead stories involve someone trying to be less dependent on someone else? All four. DeepSeek on Nvidia. OpenAI on the White House's blessing. Amazon on its own cash flow. And you, dear reader, on your browser's password manager, once Persona has your face on file. Independence is contagious this week.
