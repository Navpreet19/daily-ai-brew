Grab your coffee — today's about who's holding the chips, literally and financially, two days before Nvidia tells everyone how the quarter actually went.

## The Lead Stories

### Nine People, 130 Servers, and a Very Bad Paper Trail

Taiwan prosecutors indicted nine people — including an employee of Nvidia's Taiwan unit and two from Super Micro's Taiwan office — for breach of trust and document forgery over a scheme to illegally export high-end AI servers to China. The plan: file false paperwork claiming 130 Super Micro B300 servers, loaded with Nvidia chips covered by U.S. export controls, would stay in Taiwan. Of those, 74 were actually shipped to Chinese customers, some directly and some routed through Indonesia, Japan, and Hong Kong to muddy the trail. The remaining 56 were headed to a Japanese buyer when Taiwanese customs flagged irregularities and stopped the shipment cold.

**Bottom line:** export controls were never really a wall — they're a series of paperwork checkpoints, and this is what it looks like when nine people bet their careers that nobody would check the paperwork twice.

### Your Kindle Just Got Caught in the Memory Crossfire

Amazon quietly raised prices as much as 60% across Echo, Kindle, Fire TV, and eero devices — the Echo Dot jumped from $49.99 to $79.99, the 16GB Kindle Paperwhite from $159.99 to $199.99, the eero Pro 7 from $699.99 to $799.99. The company's stated reason: "significant increases in memory and storage component costs" it had been quietly absorbing until it couldn't anymore. It's not just Amazon — Apple, Microsoft, Dell, HP, Lenovo, and Asus have all raised prices or trimmed memory specs as the same DRAM shortage that's inflating Nvidia's server bills and Xbox prices ripples through every device with a chip in it.

**Bottom line:** the AI industry's memory shortage stopped being an abstract supply-chain story the moment it showed up as an extra thirty bucks on your Echo Dot.

### Alibaba's Answer to a Bad Earnings Report: Raise More, Ship Faster

Two days after posting a 76% profit drop blamed on AI spending, Alibaba raised $10.2 billion in a Hong Kong share placement and said all of it is going toward "full stack AI" — chips, infrastructure, and models. Then, without much of a pause, it launched Wan3.0, a video-generation model that turns documents, spreadsheets, slides, and web pages into 30-second clips. Alibaba has already committed roughly $56 billion to AI infrastructure through 2029 and is reportedly weighing raising that further; CEO Eddie Wu says he expects the capex to break even within three years.

**Bottom line:** when your earnings call spooks investors, the move isn't to spend less — it's to sell more stock and ship the product fast enough that the spending starts looking like foresight instead of panic.

### Hugging Face Tests the Market for a $13 Billion Exit

Hugging Face has hired a bank to sound out buyers for a possible sale that could value the open-source AI hub at $13 billion or more — nearly triple the $4.5 billion it fetched in its 2023 Series D, backed by Salesforce, Google, Amazon, Nvidia, and Intel. No buyer is named and no deal is signed. The timing is notable: the sale talk comes barely a month after Hugging Face said it contained a July 16 intrusion into its production infrastructure "driven, end to end, by an autonomous AI agent system" — a breach OpenAI later said came from its own cyber-capability testing.

**Bottom line:** getting hacked by a rival's AI agent apparently isn't a dealbreaker when the number on the table is triple what you were worth three years ago.

- 🕰️ OpenAI retires the o3 model from ChatGPT on August 26 — the same day Nvidia reports the earnings half the industry's been bracing for.

## The Big Picture

Zoom out and today's stories are really one story wearing four costumes: the memory and chip shortage that's been building all month has stopped staying politely inside the data center. It's in an indictment, because scarce, export-controlled hardware is worth smuggling. It's in your Echo Dot's price tag, because the components inside it got caught in the same supply crunch. And it's in the fact that two very different AI companies — one a Chinese cloud giant, one an open-source hub — both concluded the smartest move right now is to raise money while the raising's good, rather than wait and see what the crunch does to valuations later. Nvidia's earnings Wednesday will be the moment all of this either gets validated as smart positioning or exposed as everyone crowding toward the same exit at once.

## One More Thing

Industry watchers have taken to calling this stretch of chip scarcity "RAMageddon" — which is either the most on-the-nose nickname of the year or proof that everyone covering this beat has spent too many nights doomscrolling DRAM futures. Analysts now say the shortage could run until 2028. So the next time your Kindle costs forty dollars more than it should, you now have a word for who to blame, and it rhymes with Armageddon for a reason.
