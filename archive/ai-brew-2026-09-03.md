## The Lead Stories

### Nvidia Makes Its Hugging Face Rumor Official

Nvidia confirmed this week it signed a definitive agreement to acquire Hugging Face for $12.93 billion in cash and stock, ending nearly two weeks of "reportedly nears a deal" headlines with an actual press release. The chipmaker is buying access to Hugging Face's hub of roughly 3 million models, 1 million apps, half a million datasets, and 18 million developers — arguably the default home page of the open-source AI world. CEO Jensen Huang said Hugging Face will "remain an open platform for the entire AI ecosystem," and CNBC reports Hugging Face's own leadership approached Huang weeks before terms were set. The deal isn't expected to close until the first half of 2027, pending regulatory review. It's Nvidia's second-largest acquisition ever, and the stock rose more than 3% on the news.

**Bottom line:** Nvidia doesn't just want to sell the shovels anymore — it wants to own the map everyone uses to find the gold.

### The DOJ Tells the Times: That's Fair Use

The Justice Department filed a Statement of Interest this week urging a federal judge to rule that training AI models on The New York Times' copyrighted articles is fair use — the first time the federal government has formally weighed in on any of the AI copyright suits piling up against OpenAI and its peers. The brief leans on the classic transformative-use argument: a model doesn't "read" an article the way a person does, it converts it into statistical relationships about language, and that purpose is different enough from the original to count as fair use. The DOJ also framed a healthy US AI industry as a national-security interest. The filing isn't legally binding, and the Times shot back that the administration is picking sides in a case where it's reportedly also negotiating a stake in OpenAI.

**Bottom line:** the government just told AI companies they can keep training on the news — while reportedly angling to own a piece of one of them.

### Cognition Doubles Its Money for the Second Time This Year

Cognition, maker of the AI coding agent Devin, is closing in on a roughly $1 billion round that would value the company at $47 billion — up from $26 billion just three months ago, and from $10 billion at the start of the year. Bloomberg reports investor demand topped $10 billion for a round a fraction that size. The number behind the number: Cognition's annualized revenue has climbed to roughly $900 million, up from $492 million in late May, as more enterprises hand coding work to agents instead of engineers. The frenzy isn't happening in a vacuum — SpaceX's $60 billion acquisition of rival Cursor last month reset what investors think coding-agent startups are worth.

**Bottom line:** in AI coding tools right now, tripling your valuation in six months doesn't even buy you the fastest-growing line on the leaderboard.

### ChatGPT, Claude, and Grok All Called in Sick Together

For roughly two hours this morning, ChatGPT, Claude, and Grok all went down within minutes of each other — a near-simultaneous outage across three competing labs' infrastructure that's rare enough to raise eyebrows on its own. Downdetector showed error reports spiking almost in lockstep, and the disruption cascaded into tools built on top of these models, including the coding assistant Cursor. OpenAI said it was investigating elevated error rates across ChatGPT and Codex; Anthropic pinned Claude's outage on an infrastructure issue and restored service by around 12:15pm ET. None of the three companies has said whether the timing was coincidence or a shared point of failure.

**Bottom line:** the AI economy now runs enough of daily life that three unrelated outages landing in the same hour felt less like bad luck and more like a stress test everyone failed at once.

## The Big Picture

Zoom out and today's stories are about rumor finally catching up to confirmation — and what got exposed when it did. Nvidia's Hugging Face deal spent two weeks as a maybe before becoming a signed agreement; the DOJ's fair-use stance spent months as an expectation before becoming a formal filing; Cognition's valuation spent one funding cycle as speculation before becoming a term sheet. None of that confirmation slowed anything down — it just added a press release. The one story nobody saw coming was the outage, and it's the more useful data point: three competing labs' infrastructure buckling in the same ninety-minute window is a reminder that underneath every trillion-dollar valuation sits a stack nobody involved can fully explain yet.

## One More Thing

Buried under today's dollar signs: a new study published in the journal AI & Society finds that the more people interact with AI chatbots — especially in customer-service settings — the more robotic their own communication starts to sound. Researchers call it "robotoid humanness," a two-way mirroring effect where chatbots get better at sounding human right as the humans talking to them start sounding a little more like chatbots. Nobody's suggesting you're about to start speaking in bullet points and disclaimers. But the next time a friend's text reads suspiciously like a customer-service script, you now have a word for what's happening.
