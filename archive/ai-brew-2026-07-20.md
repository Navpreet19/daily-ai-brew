## Editor's Open

Monday morning and the AI world already rearranged its furniture over the weekend. China opened a new governance club nobody in Washington got invited to, Google's most-hyped model missed its own deadline for what feels like the third time, and a scrappy open-weight model out of Beijing just out-coded Anthropic's flagship on a leaderboard people actually check. Grab your coffee — we've got borders, benchmarks, and one very expensive delay to get through.

## The Lead Stories

### Beijing Starts Its Own Group Chat

At the World AI Conference in Shanghai, Xi Jinping announced the World Artificial Intelligence Cooperation Organization (WAICO) — a new intergovernmental body meant to help set global AI rules, headquartered in Shanghai. Twenty-nine countries signed on as founding members, including Russia, Brazil, Indonesia, Pakistan, Kazakhstan, and more than a dozen African and Asian nations. Notably absent: every G7 country. China also pledged 5,000 AI training and seminar slots for developing nations over the next five years. The pitch, per Xi: a "just and equitable" alternative to Western-led AI governance frameworks. It's not a treaty with teeth yet — more a flag planted — but it's the clearest signal yet that AI governance is splitting into two competing tracks instead of converging into one.

**Bottom line:** The US has spent two years writing AI export rules for its allies; China just started writing rules for everyone else.

### Google's Homework Is Late — Again

Google unveiled Gemini 3.5 Pro at its developer conference back in May and promised a June public rollout. June came and went with nothing. As of this week it's still not public, and Google hasn't named a new date. The holdup, per multiple reports: the model's coding and complex-reasoning performance fell short of Google's own internal bar during testing. The timing stings — OpenAI, xAI, and Moonshot all shipped competitive coding claims within the same three-week window. Investors noticed: Alphabet shares fell about 4.4% on the news, erasing roughly $200 billion in market value in a single session.

**Bottom line:** "Best model wins" is turning into "best model wins, on time." Being early used to be a nice-to-have — now it's priced in.

### The Open-Weight Underdog Just Became Top Dog

Moonshot AI's Kimi K3 — a 2.8-trillion-parameter model, the largest open-weight release to date — debuted at #1 on Arena.ai's Frontend Code Arena with a 1,679 Elo score, edging out Claude Fable 5 (1,631) and OpenAI's GPT-5.6 Sol (1,618). Full open weights are due by July 27; for now it's API-only. On the broader Artificial Analysis Intelligence Index it ranks fourth overall, still a notch below the very top tier — but the coding win is the headline, since coding is exactly where the labs are fighting hardest right now. It's also a pointed reminder that US compute export limits haven't stopped Chinese labs from fielding frontier-class models.

**Bottom line:** Export controls can slow the chip supply; they can't stop a determined lab from finding a leaderboard to top.

### Murati Bets Nobody Wants One-Size-Fits-All

Mira Murati's Thinking Machines shipped its first model, Inkling — a 975-billion-parameter open-weight system (mixture-of-experts, ~41 billion "active" per task), trained on 45 trillion tokens spanning text, image, audio, and video. The company is upfront that Inkling isn't the strongest model on the market; the bet is on customization instead. Full weights are on Hugging Face, and it's live for fine-tuning on Tinker, Thinking Machines' own tuning platform. A smaller sibling, Inkling-Small, is coming once testing wraps.

**Bottom line:** When you can't out-benchmark OpenAI, you out-flexible them — Inkling bets that "good enough, but yours to reshape" beats "best, but rented."

## The Big Picture

The throughline today isn't any single release — it's that the AI race just went multipolar on two axes at once. Technologically, "best model wins" is giving way to "best-fit-for-the-job wins": Kimi K3 beating Claude Fable 5 on one leaderboard doesn't make it the better model overall, it makes it the better choice for that job, at that price. Geopolitically, WAICO means there are now two AI rulebooks in draft instead of one, and the fact that zero G7 nations signed China's version tells you these aren't converging anytime soon. Layer in Google's delay — proof that even the biggest labs can't just throw compute at a deadline — and you get an industry where speed, price, and specialization matter as much as raw IQ. The "who's smartest" era is ending. Welcome to the "who's useful, on time, and to whom" era.
