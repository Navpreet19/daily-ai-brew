Everyone Shows Their Math, Some Don't Like the Grade

The Snapshot
$105B — Nvidia's finalized credit guarantee for OpenAI's new Ohio data center, down from the $250B figure floated a month ago
$65B — Anthropic's annualized revenue run rate at the end of July, up from $9B a year ago
$21B — Etched's new valuation, doubled in under a month after Jane Street tested and bought the chip startup's hardware
3B+ — global downloads of Alibaba's open-source Qwen models, more than Google and Meta combined
13–17 — the age range OpenAI's new ChatGPT for Teens, launched today, automatically routes into a locked-down mode
Tuesday's theme: every number in AI got bigger this week except the ones skeptics are watching closest.
Happy Tuesday. Nvidia's promise to bankroll OpenAI's Ohio megaproject came in $145 billion lighter than the number Wall Street had penciled in a month ago, Anthropic's revenue chart went nearly vertical again, and OpenAI shipped a version of ChatGPT built to give its youngest users a study hall instead of a rabbit hole. Different math problems, same emerging habit in an industry that's growing up fast: the numbers written on the outside of the box are finally getting checked against what's actually inside. Grab your coffee — today's about show-your-work season.

Nvidia's OpenAI IOU Shrinks by $145 Billion

Nvidia finalized its financing backstop for OpenAI's new Ohio data center at up to $105 billion in a regulatory filing Monday — well below the $250 billion figure that circulated when the partnership first leaked in July, and even below the "just under $120 billion" trial balloon reported just last week. The deal anchors an 8-gigawatt campus OpenAI is leasing from SoftBank-backed SB Energy on former federal uranium-enrichment land in Ohio's Pike County, with the first phase due online in 2028; Nvidia expects to eventually book $150-200 billion in revenue as the site's exclusive chip supplier. The signing landed the same day chip stocks slid broadly — Nvidia down roughly 2%, AMD and Intel each down about 4% — as renewed U.S.-Iran tensions pushed oil and bond yields higher and investors kept chewing on how much of the AI buildout is real demand versus vendors financing their own customers.

Bottom line: when the guarantee shrinks by nearly a third between rumor and signature, and the stock still wobbles on signing day, that's the market telling Nvidia it isn't buying the circular-financing math anymore — even at a discount.

Anthropic's Revenue Chart Forgets How to Plateau

Anthropic told investors its annualized revenue run rate topped $65 billion at the end of July, up from $47 billion in May and just $9 billion a year ago, with preliminary Q2 revenue over $11.5 billion — a fourteen-fold jump from the same quarter last year — marking the company's first quarter of positive adjusted operating income. Investors are betting the pace holds through year-end, putting Anthropic on track for $100-120 billion in 2026 revenue, the same trajectory underpinning talk of a roughly $2 trillion valuation for an IPO investors expect as soon as October, which would eclipse SpaceX's record $1.77 trillion debut. Anthropic itself hasn't confirmed an IPO timeline or size.

Bottom line: fourteen-fold revenue growth would be the headline on a normal day; the fact that a $2 trillion IPO estimate reads as plausible instead of insane tells you how unnormal this year has been.

ChatGPT Gets a Kids' Table, Whether You Ordered One or Not

OpenAI launched ChatGPT for Teens on Tuesday, a separate, more locked-down version for 13-to-17-year-olds that blocks romantic or sexual roleplay, won't claim to have feelings, and nudges a stalled homework session back into a timed Study Mode instead of just handing over the answer. Nobody has to opt in: anyone who states their age as 13-17, or whom OpenAI's age-prediction system guesses is a minor based on usage patterns like query topics and time of day, gets defaulted into it automatically, no ID required. Parents get quiet hours and alerts for flagged high-risk conversations. It's OpenAI's most concrete response yet to a year of lawsuits and scrutiny over how chatbots handle emotionally vulnerable, underage users.

Bottom line: age-guessing by behavior instead of ID is an imperfect stand-in for real verification — but after a year of teen-chatbot lawsuits, an imperfect guess beats what OpenAI had before, which was nothing.

Etched Doubles Its Price Tag Before the Ink Dries

AI inference-hardware startup Etched raised $700 million at a $21 billion valuation, more than doubling the $10.3 billion mark it set just a month ago in a $300 million round — with quant trading firm Jane Street leading the new round after first testing Etched's chips, buying them, and putting a rack to work in its own data center. Etched builds what it calls "frontier inference clusters," full systems built around chips specialized for the decode stage of running AI models rather than training them, and says it now holds more than $1 billion in signed customer contracts across AI labs and cloud providers, on top of backers including Sequoia, a16z, and Peter Thiel.

Bottom line: investors keep saying they're worried the AI infrastructure math doesn't add up, and keep writing checks that double a company's valuation in a month anyway — the caution is verbal, the money's still all-in.

  • ⚡ OpenAI is previewing "Ultrafast" mode for GPT-5.6 Sol, running up to 750 tokens per second (14x normal speed) for a small group of API customers, while free users get unlimited chats with lighter model GPT-5.6 Luna starting this week.
The Big Picture

Zoom out and today's stories are the same story told in different departments: the AI industry's numbers finally got big enough that someone's checking the math instead of just repeating it. Anthropic's revenue chart and Etched's valuation are both real, order-book-backed growth — but Nvidia's OpenAI guarantee shrinking by $145 billion between rumor and signature, and chip stocks wobbling the very day that deal got signed, is the market pricing in doubt about how much of the rest of the buildout is genuine demand versus vendors bankrolling their own customers. Even OpenAI's teen product fits the pattern in miniature: after a year of implying chatbots were basically fine for anyone, the company just shipped its first real acknowledgment that the claim needed an asterisk. Growth and scrutiny are now moving at roughly the same speed — that's new.

One More Thing

Reddit quietly started testing a "Play" mode that turns old text threads into AI-narrated short videos — synced captions, AI voices reading the original post and top comments, each one labeled "Real conversation voiced by AI." The pilot example making the rounds: an eight-year-old r/boardgames thread asking for road-trip game recommendations, now narrated like a true-crime podcast. Reddit's pitch is that it's just reclaiming the "Reddit story" video format that already draws tens of millions of views on TikTok — somewhere, a bored redditor's 2018 comment about Ticket to Ride just became content.
