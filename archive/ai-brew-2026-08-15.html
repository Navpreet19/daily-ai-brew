Everyone's Rumors Just Became Official Business

The Snapshot
$60B — SpaceX's Cursor acquisition officially closed Friday, the priciest venture-backed startup buyout in history
$2T — the IPO valuation Anthropic investors are reportedly eyeing for October, enough to eclipse SpaceX's own record debut
$500B — third-party capital Nvidia just recruited six Wall Street giants to pour into AI data centers, none of it on Nvidia's own books
$40B — the fresh valuation AI-coding rival Cognition is reportedly chasing, up from $26 billion three months ago
19% — how far behind young workers in AI-exposed jobs have fallen versus their peers, per Stanford, up from 15% a year earlier
Saturday's theme: nothing in AI stayed a rumor today — every "in talks," "reportedly," and "planned" turned into a signed check or a shipped product.

Happy Saturday — and happy Independence Day to readers in India, where Anthropic opened a Bengaluru office and a homegrown cloud company launched a sovereign AI platform on the exact 79th anniversary of the day India stopped renting its politics from London. Fitting timing, because today's whole newsletter is about renting versus owning: SpaceX bought its way into AI coding instead of building one from scratch, Nvidia offloaded half a trillion dollars of infrastructure risk onto Wall Street instead of its own balance sheet, and Anthropic's investors started attaching a number to an IPO the company hasn't even confirmed it wants. Grab your coffee — today the rumors clocked out and the receipts clocked in.

SpaceX Hits Ctrl+Buy on the AI Coding Wars

SpaceX's acquisition of AI coding startup Cursor became official Friday, according to a regulatory filing — the largest acquisition of a venture-backed startup in history, closing two months after the all-stock, $60 billion deal was first announced. Cursor, whose AI code editor crossed $1 billion in annualized revenue by last November, now sits inside the merged SpaceX-xAI operation alongside Grok. SpaceX has said Cursor's built-in audience of expert software engineers mattered as much as its technology — a distribution shortcut into the developer market that Grok hadn't cracked on its own. The timing isn't coincidental: rival coding startup Cognition, maker of the AI software engineer Devin, is separately in talks for a new round that could value it near $40 billion, up from $26 billion just three months ago.

Bottom line: Musk didn't build Grok a coding audience — he bought one, for the price of a mid-size country's GDP, and made sure a rival was scrambling to match him within days.

Nvidia Finds Six Roommates for Its $500 Billion Habit

Nvidia said this week it's partnering with Apollo, BlackRock, Blackstone, Brookfield, Goldman Sachs, and KKR to build financing platforms designed to mobilize more than $500 billion in outside capital for AI data centers, power, and infrastructure — money that funds the buildings without ever touching Nvidia's own balance sheet. Under the agreements, the five asset managers will deploy long-duration institutional and insurance capital while Goldman leads public debt offerings; CEO Jensen Huang said Nvidia approached exactly six firms, and all six said yes. The pitch: treat Nvidia compute like an investable asset class, the way investors already treat office towers or toll roads, so hyperscalers and AI labs can keep buying chips without every dollar showing up on Nvidia's own books.

Bottom line: Nvidia just convinced six of Wall Street's more risk-averse investors to bankroll its customers' shopping list — which tells you who's actually expected to hold the bag if this buildout doesn't pay off, and it isn't Nvidia.

India Marks 79 Years of Independence by Declaring an AI One Too

On the 79th anniversary of independence from British rule, India spent Saturday shipping AI sovereignty instead of just discussing it. Anthropic opened a Bengaluru office, noting India is now Claude's second-largest market worldwide, with Tata Consultancy Services training 50,000 employees on the model. Separately, homegrown provider Island Computing launched what it's calling India's first fully managed sovereign cloud — data centers, hardware, and identity systems all operated on Indian soil, aimed at AI, SaaS, and regulated workloads. It's the latest chapter in a buildout that accelerated after the U.S. government forced Anthropic to cut off Indian access to its most capable models back in June, a shock that's since produced open-source models trained end-to-end domestically and a nine-institution consortium covering all 22 scheduled languages.

Bottom line: India spent decades importing its technology; the country that just watched Washington flip an off-switch on its AI access isn't interested in ever being that dependent again.

Anthropic's Investors Want a Bigger Trophy Than SpaceX's

A day after OpenAI revealed its revenue run rate had topped $40 billion, Anthropic investors are reportedly circling an even bigger number: a roughly $2 trillion valuation for an IPO they expect as soon as October, which would top SpaceX's record-setting $1.77 trillion debut and become the largest public listing ever. The figure isn't coming from Anthropic itself — executives haven't set an official target even privately — but from investors extrapolating off the company's growth, betting annual revenue could reach $100-120 billion by year's end, more than tenfold higher than a year ago. Anthropic isn't without headwinds, including cheaper Chinese rivals, regulatory friction, and fallout from that same government-ordered access ban that just pushed India toward AI self-reliance.

Bottom line: when your investors are drafting your IPO valuation before you've confirmed you're even going public, that's either supreme confidence or a warning sign — probably both.

The Big Picture

Zoom out and today's stories all describe the same shift: hedging turning into commitment. Nvidia stopped promising infrastructure would get built and started signing the checks that guarantee it, routed through six of Wall Street's biggest balance sheets instead of its own. SpaceX stopped talking about buying Cursor and now owns it outright. India stopped drafting sovereign-AI policy papers and opened a cloud platform with servers running today, on a date chosen for maximum symbolism. And Anthropic's investors stopped whispering about an eventual IPO and started attaching a $2 trillion price tag to one that isn't even confirmed. None of this proves the AI boom is right about its own math — it proves the industry has decided the debating phase is over, and everyone's placing real money on their answer.

One More Thing

Buried in today's open-source model drop: Alibaba's new Qwen3.8-27B is small enough to run locally on a single high-end gaming graphics card, while landing within shouting distance of last year's frontier models on coding tests. A few years ago, that kind of capability needed a data center. Now it needs a gaming PC and a decent power supply — somewhere, a computer science student just canceled a very expensive cloud bill.
