## The Lead Stories

### Nvidia Aced the Test America's Been Sweating For

Nvidia beat on every line Wednesday afternoon: $96.2 billion in revenue (up about 106% year over year) against a Street looking for roughly $92 billion, with data-center revenue alone hitting $89 billion versus estimates near $85 billion. Adjusted earnings came in at $2.22 a share versus the $2.09 expected, and the company guided next quarter's revenue to $108 billion, comfortably above the roughly $104 billion Wall Street had penciled in. The stock, which had drifted lower in eight of the previous nine trading sessions heading into the print, jumped as much as 4% in after-hours trading on the news. The one asterisk: next quarter's gross-margin guide came in at 74%, down from 75% this quarter — the first sequential dip of the entire AI buildout, as memory and infrastructure costs even Nvidia can't fully dodge start showing up on its own books.

**Bottom line:** the company that's been the industry's report card for two years just proved the demand is real — but even Nvidia can't out-guide the memory shortage forever.

### OpenAI's Homemade Chip Crashes Nvidia's Earnings Party

One day before Nvidia's earnings, OpenAI published the first public benchmarks for its in-house inference chip, codenamed Jalapeño and co-developed with Broadcom and Celestica, claiming it delivers 1.5x to 1.9x more AI work per watt than Nvidia's Blackwell and upcoming Rubin systems, with up to 3.6x lower latency. The tests ran on SemiAnalysis's independent InferenceX benchmark across three open models, with SemiAnalysis verifying some runs on-site — an attempt to head off the usual skepticism around vendor-published numbers. Jalapeño only handles inference, not training, and won't deploy inside OpenAI's own infrastructure until later this year, with two more chip generations already in the works. The timing lines up with Anthropic's own hire this week of a veteran Google TPU architect — both labs are racing to own the hardware their models actually run on.

**Bottom line:** Nvidia's biggest customers keep buying its chips and building the case for why they might not need to, in the same week.

### Amazon Pulls the Plug on the Original "Fake" AI

Amazon will shut down Mechanical Turk on September 30, ending a 21-year run for the crowdsourced task marketplace Jeff Bezos once called "artificial artificial intelligence" — a wink at the fact it was humans, not algorithms, doing the labeling, transcribing, and surveying underneath the hood. The platform once counted more than 500,000 workers and quietly helped train a generation of the machine-learning models built on top of that human-labeled data. Amazon pointed to how far AI capability has come, plus growing competition from dedicated data-labeling platforms; it stopped onboarding new customers last month. The company didn't mention it, but a 2023 study found nearly half of surveyed Turk workers had already started using AI tools to complete the very tasks meant to train AI.

**Bottom line:** the platform built to fake artificial intelligence just got shut down by the real thing.

### ChatGPT Quietly Ghosted Reddit

Reddit's share of ChatGPT Search citations fell 86% in just four days this month, according to tracking firm Promptwatch — from an average of 3.83% of citations in late July to about 0.52% by mid-August. The likely trigger: OpenAI changed how ChatGPT generates its background search queries around August 8, with the share of searches using a site-specific operator jumping from roughly 0.4% to nearly 17% of all queries and climbing since. Reddit's citation drop on Google's AI Overviews and AI Mode over the same stretch was far milder — down 11% and 30% respectively — pointing to something specific about ChatGPT's retrieval behavior rather than a broader shift away from citing forums. OpenAI hasn't explained the change publicly.

**Bottom line:** when one company's unannounced tweak can erase most of your AI visibility overnight, staying visible now means keeping an algorithm's trust you can't see or audit.

## The Big Picture

Zoom out and today's stories are two separate arguments — "is the AI boom's demand real" and "is the AI boom's supply chain fragile" — both getting fresh evidence, pointing the same direction. Nvidia's beat says the buying keeps coming; its shaved-down margin guide says even Nvidia is starting to feel the memory and infrastructure costs it usually passes upstream. OpenAI's chip benchmarks and Anthropic's TPU hire this week say the biggest labs no longer trust the market to solve that supply problem for them — they're each quietly building a way around Nvidia's queue instead. And underneath it all, the tools this boom is built on keep eating the last pieces of the human labor that trained them, from Mechanical Turk's shutdown to an attribution economy so opaque that a single unannounced tweak can erase a decade of a website's visibility overnight. The industry isn't choosing between growth and its own limits anymore — it's just moving fast enough that both show up in the same week.

## One More Thing

Quick trivia to send you off: Amazon named Mechanical Turk after an 18th-century chess-playing automaton that wowed European courts for decades before it turned out to have a hidden human operator folded inside the cabinet. Two centuries later, Amazon built a platform whose entire premise was hidden humans doing the "artificial" intelligence — and this week, actual AI finally made even that redundant. History doesn't just rhyme; apparently it also eventually gets acquired.
