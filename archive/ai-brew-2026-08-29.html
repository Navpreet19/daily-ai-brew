Friday's theme: everyone in AI spent the week building bigger, then spent Friday quietly checking their own brakes.

Editor's Open

Happy Friday. Nvidia closed out its best week ever by pumping the brakes on itself — pausing a financing deal its own lawyers got nervous about before regulators had to ask. Salesforce decided the smartest move for its own AI was betting on someone else's. And Meta patched a privacy hole in its glasses for the second time since July. Grab your coffee — today's about what it looks like when an industry starts self-correcting slightly faster than anyone's forcing it to.

Nvidia's Financing Program Gets Cold Feet

Nvidia has paused parts of a financing initiative it launched July 1 that offered credit support to smaller "neocloud" AI providers — think CoreWeave, Nebius, IREN — in exchange for a cut of their revenue and a promise to rent unused GPU capacity itself if a provider couldn't find another customer, the Wall Street Journal reported Thursday. The reason, per people familiar with the matter: some Nvidia employees grew worried the arrangement, which let Nvidia dictate elements of how customers ran their businesses, could draw antitrust scrutiny. An Nvidia spokesperson said the model is still in place and "continues to evolve," and the company could revive or fold it into something else later. Neocloud stocks fell Friday on the news, with IREN down more than 7% before a partial rebound, CoreWeave and Nebius also sliding.

Bottom line: the company selling the shovels just found out that owning a piece of the bank too might get you sued — a fitting gut check in the same week Nvidia posted one of the best quarters any company has ever had.

Salesforce Bets Big on Someone Else's Model

Salesforce beat Wall Street's Q2 targets Wednesday — $11.35 billion in revenue, $5.90 in adjusted earnings per share against a $3.27 estimate — and the stock jumped as much as 22% before settling around a 14% pop, one of its biggest post-earnings moves in years. Dig into that EPS beat, though, and $2.6 billion of the $3.53 billion in net income came from investment gains, including Salesforce's own stake in Anthropic, not from selling more software. The headline landed alongside earnings: "Claudeforce," a sweeping new partnership making Anthropic's Claude the default reasoning engine across Salesforce's stack, including a new plugin with 37 prebuilt sales skills that lets reps work inside Claude instead of Salesforce's own app. Agentforce and Data 360 combined are already at $3.9 billion in annual recurring revenue, up more than 210% year over year.

Bottom line: when the CRM giant decides its own chatbot isn't where the future happens, "AI-native" stops being a marketing line and starts being an admission.

Meta Closes the Loophole on Its Peeping Glasses

Meta is rolling out a fix, announced Thursday by AR vice president Alex Himel, that shuts off recording on its Ray-Ban and Oakley smart glasses the instant someone covers the capture-indicator light mid-video — closing a gap where the camera previously only checked the light before recording started, not during. It's Meta's second privacy patch in under two months, following a July update that disabled the camera entirely if the LED was physically damaged. Neither update addresses the underlying complaint that's dogged the product: people keep using the glasses to record others without consent in public, and a lit LED only protects bystanders who know to look for it in the first place.

Bottom line: Meta keeps patching the hardware tell instead of the human behavior it's supposed to warn people about — a fix for the how, not the why.

Quick Hits

  • 📉 Marvell shares fell about 8% Friday even after beating on revenue and raising guidance, after CEO Matt Murphy told investors the bulk of its Google custom-chip revenue won't land until fiscal 2029 — later than markets had priced in.
  • 💸 OpenAI cut GPT-5.6 Sol API pricing by up to 33% through November 21, the third price cut on that model line in a month, as it fends off cheaper Chinese open models and Anthropic's own rates.
  • 🌎 Alibaba Cloud opened its first two data centers in Brazil, its initial infrastructure footprint in South America and the latest front in China's push to export its cloud stack alongside Washington's.
  • 🤝 Nvidia's reported $12.9 billion Hugging Face buyout is still unconfirmed by either company three days after it first leaked, even as outlets keep digging into what a chipmaker owning AI's "neutral" model hub would mean for developers on rival hardware.
  • 📚 Google launched "Expert Intelligence" in Gemini Notebook, letting users load purchased Google Play ebooks — over 100,000 titles at launch, from publishers including Penguin Random House and O'Reilly — straight into notebooks to query and study, with wider rollout to the Gemini app coming soon.
The Big Picture

Zoom out and Friday reads like the industry's nervous system firing back at itself. Nvidia spent Thursday posting one of the best quarters any company has ever had, then spent Friday quietly stepping back from a financing scheme its own employees worried looked too much like market control — self-correcting before regulators had to ask. Salesforce's blowout quarter told a similar story from the demand side: the company decided its own AI wasn't good enough to keep customers inside its own app, handed the wheel to a rival's model instead, and investors rewarded the humility with a 20%-plus pop. And Meta, for the second time in two months, patched a privacy hole in its glasses before a lawsuit or a new law forced its hand. None of these companies got more cautious by choice — antitrust lawyers, model-agnostic customers, and harassment headlines did that work for them. The boom isn't slowing down; it's just started building its own guardrails slightly faster than Washington can build its.

One More Thing

Buried in the Hugging Face frenzy: NBA star Kevin Durant and his business partner Rich Kleiman put a combined $250,000 into Hugging Face's 2018 seed and 2019 Series A rounds through their investment fund. If Nvidia's reported $12.9 billion price holds and Durant's stake wasn't diluted away over eight years of follow-on rounds, that's an estimated $60 million-plus payout — a better return than anything he's done on a basketball court, and a reminder that in this market, the smartest investment of 2018 apparently wore high-tops.
