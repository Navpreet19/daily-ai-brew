Thursday means one thing this week: GPT-5.6 is finally, actually, for-real public. But the more interesting story might be happening 7,000 miles away, where a Chinese food-delivery company just proved you can train a trillion-parameter model without a single Nvidia chip in the building. Grab your coffee — today's about who gets to build the future, and increasingly, who gets to build it cheap.

## The Lead Stories

### Sol Finally Rises

OpenAI's GPT-5.6 family — Sol, Terra, and Luna — goes fully public today, ending three weeks of on-again, off-again access after the Trump administration first paused the release over national-security concerns, then cleared it Wednesday following what OpenAI describes as additional government testing. Sol is OpenAI's strongest model yet, with gains in coding, biology, and cybersecurity benchmarks; Terra matches GPT-5.5's performance at roughly half the price; Luna undercuts both as OpenAI's cheapest model yet. Pricing lands at $5 per million input tokens and $30 per million output for Sol, with Terra and Luna scaled down from there. It's the second frontier model in two weeks — after Anthropic's Fable 5 — to get gated, reviewed, then released.

**Bottom line:** A model that was too risky to ship three weeks ago is fine today, and the difference wasn't the model — it was a few meetings in Washington. That's quietly become how frontier AI launches work now.

### A Delivery App Just Out-Trained the Frontier

Chinese food-delivery giant Meituan open-sourced LongCat-2.0 this week, a 1.6-trillion-parameter agentic coding model trained from scratch on a cluster of 50,000 Chinese-made AI chips — not a single Nvidia GPU involved, according to Meituan, with domestic chipmakers Huawei, Moore Threads, and MetaX confirming their hardware supports the system. Released free under the MIT license, LongCat-2.0 reportedly beats GPT-5.5 on the SWE-bench Pro coding benchmark and has already climbed to the top of OpenRouter's usage charts. It's the largest model anyone has trained entirely on non-Nvidia silicon at this scale.

**Bottom line:** Yesterday it was a Chinese lab reportedly designing its own chip. Today it's a food-delivery company shipping a frontier-class model built entirely on chips that already exist. The "China can't do frontier AI without Nvidia" argument just got a lot harder to make.

### China's Discount Models Are Eating Everyone's Lunch

Chinese AI models now account for as much as 46% of the tokens U.S. developers push through OpenRouter, a popular model-routing platform, up from an average of just 11% over the prior year, CNBC reported this week citing OpenRouter data. The draw is blunt: open-source Chinese models run 60-90% cheaper than comparable offerings from OpenAI and Anthropic, and platforms increasingly route routine tasks to "good enough" cheap models rather than pay frontier prices for every request. Z.ai's GLM 5.2, released in June, saw daily token volume grow roughly 27x in its first week alone.

**Bottom line:** OpenAI and Anthropic can still win on capability. But for the large chunk of AI work that doesn't need the best model — just a good-enough one — price is becoming the whole pitch, and China's currently winning it.

### Prime Intellect Wants You to Bring Your Own Compute

Prime Intellect raised a $130 million Series A at a $1 billion valuation, led by Radical Ventures with Nvidia Ventures and Intel Capital among the backers, TechCrunch reported Wednesday. The startup sells compute and tooling that let companies train and run their own AI agents without depending on a frontier lab's API — a pitch that's found real traction, with annualized revenue already around $100 million. The round drew a notable angel list, including Perplexity's Aravind Srinivas, Box's Aaron Levie, Harvey's Winston Weinberg, and Mercor's Brendan Foody.

**Bottom line:** Even as OpenAI and Anthropic push frontier models that cost billions to train, there's a growing market for companies who'd rather own their AI stack outright than rent someone else's. Sovereignty isn't just a national concern anymore — it's a line item on startup cap tables too.

⚖️ The FTC opened public comment (through July 31) on a policy statement addressing state laws that force AI models to alter "truthful outputs," following a December executive order from President Trump.

## The Big Picture

Zoom out from today's four stories and they're all pointing at the same fracture: the AI industry is splitting into an "own everything" track and a "rent the frontier" track, and the "own it" side just had a very good week. Meituan owns its chips. Prime Intellect's customers want to own their compute. Chinese developers are increasingly happy to own a slightly-worse-but-way-cheaper model instead of renting GPT-5.6 by the token. OpenAI's counter-bet is that Sol is worth paying for — today's launch is essentially a wager that frontier capability alone can outrun the pull toward independence. That's the real race now: not who has the smartest model, but who needs the least permission to use one.
