# The AI Brew • August 11, 2026 • The AI Boom Just Found Its Landlords

- **$15B** — Intel's first stock sale since its 1971 IPO, cashing in a 175% rally this year to fund its AI comeback
- **100%** — the share of local electricity-bill hikes Anthropic just promised to personally cover for neighbors of its new data centers
- **95%** — GPT-5.6-Cyber's hit rate on advanced hacking simulations, versus 1.5% for OpenAI's standard model

## Editor's Open

Happy Tuesday. In the span of about twelve hours on Monday, Nvidia recruited six of Wall Street's biggest money managers into a $500 billion financing coalition, Anthropic found its own landlord in a Singaporean sovereign wealth fund, and Intel sold stock for the first time since Nixon was in office. If Monday had one theme, it's that nobody wants to finance the AI boom with their own cash anymore — everyone's shopping for someone else's balance sheet. Grab your coffee; today's about who's actually footing the bill.

## The Lead Stories

### Nvidia Just Deputized Half of Wall Street

Nvidia announced Monday it's partnering with six of the largest alternative-asset managers — Apollo, BlackRock, Blackstone, Brookfield, Goldman Sachs, and KKR — to build independent financing platforms aimed at mobilizing more than $500 billion in third-party capital for AI infrastructure: chips, power generation, data centers. CEO Jensen Huang told CNBC he personally called only those six firms, and "none of them turned me down." The deals are still MOUs, not signed checks — final structuring and terms are yet to come — but the intent is clear: Nvidia wants outside capital, not its own balance sheet, financing the buildout its GPU sales depend on. Investors weren't uniformly thrilled; Nvidia shares still slipped nearly 3% Monday amid broader market jitters over oil prices.

**Bottom line:** Nvidia just recruited the same firms that fund highways and pipelines to fund AI's plumbing — a sign the industry now treats data centers as infrastructure, not R&D.

### Anthropic Finds a Landlord Too — and Promises to Pay Your Power Bill

Anthropic unveiled its own infrastructure venture the same day: a new platform called Theseus Infrastructure, built with Macquarie Asset Management and Singapore's $1.2 trillion sovereign wealth fund GIC, that will develop, own, and lease dedicated data centers to Anthropic under long-term contracts, with Macquarie and GIC funding the majority of each project's equity. The notable part is the fine print — Anthropic pledged to cover 100% of local grid-upgrade costs and any resulting spike in nearby residents' electricity bills, a direct response to the community backlash that's already stalled data-center approvals in several US states. The venture starts with US sites and builds on Anthropic's 2025 pledge to spend $50 billion on custom data centers in Texas and New York.

**Bottom line:** paying the neighbors' power bill isn't generosity — it's the toll AI labs are learning they have to pay to keep building at all.

### Intel Sells Stock for the First Time Since Nixon Was President

Intel opened a $15 billion common stock offering Monday — its first public share sale since its 1971 IPO — capitalizing on a stock price that's surged 175% this year and roughly quintupled over the past twelve months on AI-fueled demand. The preliminary offering doesn't yet set a price or share count, and underwriters have a 30-day option to buy up to $2.25 billion more. Intel says proceeds go toward "general corporate purposes," which the company has signaled means capital expenditure on chip manufacturing and new AI-silicon products, as customers keep signaling what Intel calls "unprecedented" demand for AI compute.

**Bottom line:** after years of playing catch-up to Nvidia and AMD, Intel is cashing in its comeback rally to buy itself more time — and more fabs.

### OpenAI Built an AI That's Great at Hacking, on Purpose

OpenAI expanded its Daybreak program Monday into two tiers — Daybreak Blue, open access minus system-level cyber guardrails, and the vetted-only Daybreak Red — and released GPT-5.6-Cyber, a version trained specifically to find zero-day vulnerabilities and build exploit chains. On OpenAI's own benchmark, GPT-5.6-Cyber answers 95% of advanced cyberattack prompts that standard Sol answers correctly only 1.5% of the time, though it actually scores worse afterward at writing up vulnerability reports. OpenAI frames the release as arming defenders faster than attackers can weaponize AI on their own; the model is rated "High" for cyber capability under the company's Preparedness Framework, just below the "Critical" threshold that would trigger tighter controls.

**Bottom line:** OpenAI just admitted the cyber-defense race is close enough that it's willing to hand vetted defenders an AI hacking tool — betting the good guys reach it first.

## Quick Hits

- 🧠 Meta released Muse Glimmer, a 30-billion-parameter open-weight model (Apache 2.0) distilled from its bigger Muse Spark, small enough with 4-bit quantization to run under 20GB on a single consumer GPU — Meta says a beefier Muse Spark 1.2 is coming next.
- 🛡️ Corma, a Tel Aviv- and San Francisco-based startup building what it calls the first foundation model for defensive cybersecurity, emerged from stealth with a $60 million seed round led by Sequoia, plus Khosla Ventures and Coatue.
- 📉 Nvidia shares fell almost 3% Monday even as the $500 billion financing news broke, though Bank of America still calls the stock "cheap" relative to its growth.
- 🌏 Singapore's GIC — now sized at roughly $1.2 trillion — becomes one of the first sovereign wealth funds directly bankrolling a frontier AI lab's own data centers, not just its chipmakers.
- 📊 The 2026 layoffs tally passed 322 events and 205,832 workers as of Monday, with AI or automation cited in more than half of them, according to one running tracker.

## The Big Picture

Zoom out and Monday was the day AI infrastructure finance grew up. Nvidia, Anthropic, and Intel all moved money in the same 24 hours, but none of them moved only their own money — Nvidia recruited six asset managers, Anthropic recruited an infrastructure fund and a sovereign wealth fund, and Intel tapped public shareholders instead of burning its own cash reserves. That's a shift from a year when labs and hyperscalers largely self-funded with their own balance sheets to one where AI capex increasingly looks like project finance: dedicated vehicles, long-term leases, outside capital absorbing the risk. It's also, not coincidentally, the same day Anthropic promised to personally cover strangers' electricity bills — a sign the money now comes with community-relations strings attached. The AI boom isn't slowing down; it's just spreading the bill around.

## One More Thing

An independent researcher named Shrivu Shankar spent his week quietly interrogating chatbots about their own birthdays. By quizzing frontier models on niche historical facts and self-reported dates, he found Anthropic's Opus 4.7-and-up models share a training cutoff around late December 2025, OpenAI's GPT-5.6 family clusters around late February 2026 — and Opus 5, despite a published May 2026 cutoff, seems to know less about the world than its official birthday would suggest. Even the AI models, apparently, fudge their resumes.
