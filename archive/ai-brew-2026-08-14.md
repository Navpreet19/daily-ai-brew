## Editor's Open

Happy Friday. Apple just became the first foreign company Beijing has ever cleared to run its own AI model inside China — built, tellingly, with help from Alibaba, the local giant Apple usually treats as a supplier, not a co-pilot. That's the kind of quiet, structural move that matters more than any single funding round, even in a week stuffed with them. Grab your coffee — today's about who's getting bigger, who's getting cheaper, and who's paying more for the privilege either way.

## The Lead Stories

### Apple Builds China Its Own Brain

Apple has trained a large language model specifically for the Chinese market with support from Alibaba, a departure from its usual approach of licensing outside models for local markets, sources told multiple outlets this week. The arrangement reportedly makes Apple the first foreign company Beijing has cleared to deploy its own proprietary AI model inside China, following a Cyberspace Administration approval last month. Alibaba's Qwen technology underpins the integration, which is expected to bring Apple Intelligence to iPhones, iPads, and Macs in China via an upcoming iOS update — a market where Apple has struggled to match Huawei's homegrown AI features.

**Bottom line:** Apple spent years resisting a China-specific AI strategy; getting Beijing's blessing to run its own model, built with a local rival's help, tells you how badly it needed one.

### Databricks Wanted $1 Billion. It Got $15 Billion.

Databricks closed a $5 billion funding round Thursday at a $190 billion valuation — its second raise this year, up from $134 billion just six months ago — led by Coatue, Blackstone, MGX, and T. Rowe Price. CEO Ali Ghodsi told TechCrunch the company only wanted to raise $1 billion until a leak about the fundraise forced its hand mid-conference, after which investor interest ballooned to $15 billion. The raise lands alongside news that Databricks' revenue run rate crossed $7 billion, up more than 80% year-over-year, as enterprises keep paying to wrangle their data before feeding it to AI models.

**Bottom line:** when a company undersells its own raise by 15x and investors still show up, that's not hype — that's a queue.

### Uber Recruits a Chinese Robotaxi Company to Take Europe

Uber and Pony.ai announced Thursday they're expanding their robotaxi partnership beyond its first commercial launch in Zagreb to more than 2,000 vehicles across four additional European cities, with more planned for the Middle East. Pony.ai supplies the Level 4 autonomous driving technology and vehicle operations; Uber supplies the riders, through the same app millions already use to hail a human-driven car. It's the clearest sign yet that autonomous ride-hailing in Europe won't be built city by city from scratch — it'll be assembled from whichever AV company already has the tech working somewhere else, regardless of where that somewhere else is.

**Bottom line:** Europe wasn't ready to build its own robotaxi fleet, so it's renting one from the same country supplying much of its EV supply chain.

### OpenAI's Revenue Doubles, IPO Clock Ticks Louder

OpenAI's annualized revenue run rate has topped $40 billion, roughly double where it stood at the end of 2025, Bloomberg reported Thursday, citing internal figures. Growth has been driven by Codex and other coding tools, subscription sales, and OpenAI's nascent advertising business; President Greg Brockman reportedly told staff the run rate grew more than 20% month-over-month in July alone. The milestone lands as OpenAI reportedly prepares for a public listing that could value the company north of $1 trillion — and as the company simultaneously swapped chief revenue officers, replacing Denise Dresser with former Wiz president Dali Rajic.

**Bottom line:** doubling your revenue in eight months is the kind of number that makes "IPO whenever we feel like it" start to sound like "IPO soon, actually."

## Quick Hits

- 💸 DeepSeek is hiking prices on its flagship V4 Pro model by more than 4x at peak hours starting Sunday — the same week Google cut Gemini 3.7 Flash's launch price in half.
- ⚡ OpenAI previewed "Ultrafast," a new API tier that runs GPT-5.6 Sol up to 14x faster (750 tokens/second) using Cerebras chips, currently testing with Jane Street and a handful of other early customers.
- 🏭 SMIC posted record $3 billion quarterly revenue and is raising wafer prices for the first time in years, as AI-chip orders outrun the Chinese foundry's capacity additions.
- 🤖 SpaceXAI, the fully merged xAI-SpaceX entity, shipped Grok 4.6 this week, a long-context model that now matches OpenAI's GPT-5.6 Sol on independent benchmarks.
- 🎓 Google rolled out Gemini 3.7 Flash, its cheapest and most capable coding-and-agent model yet, just three weeks after its last release.

## The Big Picture

Zoom out and today's stories split into two currents that are actually the same story. On one side: money and reach keep concentrating upward — OpenAI's revenue is compounding fast enough to double in eight months, Databricks investors offered fifteen times more than it asked for, Apple just got permission to plant its flag inside the one major market it couldn't previously touch, and Uber is scaling autonomous rides across a continent by renting someone else's robots. On the other: the layer everyone actually pays for — a token, a query, a ride — is being repriced in opposite directions at once, DeepSeek up, Google down, depending on who's racing for speed and who's racing for volume. Scale is getting easier to buy. What scale costs you, day to day, is getting harder to predict.

## One More Thing

Buried in Databricks' funding news: CEO Ali Ghodsi told reporters this week that, in his view, AGI has already arrived — not as one dramatic moment, but as the quiet accumulation of AI systems already doing real economic work at scale. No word yet on whether the artificial general intelligence has been informed of its own arrival, or whether it, too, thinks $190 billion sounds about right.
