# The AI Brew • September 8, 2026 • Beijing Goes All In While Anthropic Plays It Cool

## The Snapshot

- **$532B** — China's five-year infrastructure pledge to nearly quintuple national AI computing capacity by 2030
- **€21B** — Mistral's new valuation after a Samsung-led raise, the largest tech round any European company has ever closed
- **$48B** — Cognition's valuation after its funding round doubled in size in the space of a few days
- **$6B** — the Decart acquisition Anthropic walked away from, the same day its own IPO got bumped to mid-October
- **1.653 seconds** — how long MiniMax's newest video model takes to generate five seconds of footage, faster than the clip plays back
- *Tuesday's theme: everybody's writing enormous checks today — the only real news is which ones got torn up.*

## Editor's Open

Happy Tuesday. In one 24-hour stretch, China's industrial ministry pledged over half a trillion dollars to out-build the chip restrictions Washington keeps threatening, Mistral turned itself into Europe's best-funded startup ever, and Cognition watched its own funding round double before the ink dried — while Anthropic, of all labs, spent the same day saying no to a $6 billion deal and pushing its own blockbuster IPO back a month. Grab your coffee: today's about who's spending fast, who's spending carefully, and why those turned out to be very different companies.

## The Lead Stories

### Beijing Puts a Price Tag on Not Needing You

China's Ministry of Industry and Information Technology published its 15th Five-Year Plan for the information and communications sector this week, targeting 9,800 exaflops of "intelligent computing capacity" by 2030 — nearly 4.5 times the 2,185 exaflops the country had as of June, itself up 177% from a year earlier. The plan commits ¥3.8 trillion ($532 billion) in cumulative information-infrastructure spending through 2030, calls for "orderly deployment" of GPU clusters running anywhere from 10,000 to 100,000-plus accelerator cards, and explicitly pushes for that infrastructure to run on homegrown chips rather than imported ones. It's Beijing's clearest numeric answer yet to a summer of U.S. chip-export fights: rather than keep lobbying for access to Nvidia's best silicon, the plan just budgets to out-build the restriction entirely.

**Bottom line:** you can't sanction your way around a country that's willing to spend half a trillion dollars proving it doesn't need your chips.

### Mistral Cashes In on Being Europe's Best Answer

Paris-based Mistral AI raised €3 billion in a Series D round led by Samsung Electronics, valuing the company above €21 billion — nearly double the €11.7 billion it fetched a year ago, and the largest single equity round any European tech company has ever closed. New investors including Luxembourg's sovereign fund, BlackRock-managed accounts, and Advent joined returning backers a16z, ASML, General Catalyst, Lightspeed, Nvidia, and Salesforce Ventures. The money is earmarked for compute capacity and international expansion, as Mistral leans further into pitching itself as the "sovereign" AI option for governments and companies wary of routing everything through Silicon Valley or Beijing.

**Bottom line:** when a government's own money shows up in your Series D, "sovereign AI" stops being a marketing line and starts being a customer base with an army attached.

### Cognition's Round Grew Up Mid-Negotiation

Coding-agent startup Cognition closed a $2 billion funding round at a $48 billion valuation, Bloomberg reported — roughly double the size of the $1 billion at $47 billion investors were reportedly still negotiating with the company just days earlier, after Cognition fielded more than $10 billion in demand. Andreessen Horowitz, Accel, Founders Fund, General Catalyst, and Avenir led the round. Cognition's annual recurring revenue has more than doubled since it acquired rival coding tool Windsurf in July, and AI coding agents broadly have become the most reliably fundable corner of the industry right now, even as questions persist about how differentiated any of them stay once the underlying frontier models keep improving on their own.

**Bottom line:** investors didn't just meet Cognition's ask this week, they doubled it — which says more about how afraid money is of missing the coding-agent trade than about anything Cognition proved in the meantime.

### Anthropic Says No to One Deal, Not Yet to Another

Anthropic walked away from a roughly $6 billion deal to acquire Israeli AI-infrastructure startup Decart after completing due diligence, Bloomberg reported — a deal that would have been the largest acquisition in Anthropic's history, built around Decart's technology for squeezing more inference out of existing chips. Neither company has said publicly why talks collapsed, though the timing sits awkwardly close to scrutiny over Qatar's investment in Anthropic and questions about a Qatari-linked buyer acquiring an Israeli company. Separately, Reuters reported the same day that Anthropic is pushing its own IPO — one bankers hope could value the company north of $2 trillion — back to mid-October at the earliest, alongside a $15 billion credit facility, landing just days before the U.S. midterms.

**Bottom line:** it's an odd 24 hours for a company to cancel a $6 billion purchase and delay a possible $2 trillion sale of itself, but "move carefully with other people's money" has described a lot of what Anthropic's done all year.

## The Big Picture

Zoom out and today's money split into two very different instincts. China's answer to a summer of chip-export threats wasn't a diplomatic push, it was a half-trillion-dollar spending commitment — the state deciding that scale itself is the countermeasure. Mistral and Cognition show the private-market version of the same instinct: when investors think they might miss the next big compute or coding platform, they don't just meet the ask, they double it in a matter of days. And then there's Anthropic, sitting on both the biggest expected IPO in history and a mountain of new compute commitments, choosing this exact week to say no to a $6 billion deal and buy itself another month before going public. None of that is caution in the ordinary sense — a $2 trillion listing isn't a cautious company. It's a reminder that even in a market where everyone else is racing to write the bigger check, the smartest money sometimes spends its energy deciding what not to buy.

## One More Thing

Buried under today's checks and pledges: MiniMax released Sol-H3, a new inference stack for its H3 video model that generates five seconds of 1344×768 video with stereo audio in 1.653 seconds on a single 8-GPU Nvidia B300 system — up to 15.5 times faster than the model it's built on. Read that again: the AI can now generate a five-second video faster than it takes to actually watch the five seconds. Somewhere, a film editor is having an existential crisis about render times that no longer exist.
