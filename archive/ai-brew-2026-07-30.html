AI Asks for Brakes While the Money Keeps Flooring It

The Snapshot
$35B — Moonshot AI's new valuation after a $3.5B round led by Beijing's state AI fund, more than double what the Kimi K3 maker was targeting
1,100+ — AI workers, including Anthropic's and OpenAI's own leadership, who signed a letter asking Washington to help build the brakes for AI development
$14B — price tag on Meta and BlackRock's new AI data center joint venture in El Paso, Texas
-2.3% — Nvidia's Tuesday stock slide, even as the chipmaker throws off $48.5B in quarterly free cash flow
$130B–$145B — Meta's newly raised 2026 capex range, up from just $72.2B last year
Today's theme: everyone in AI wants guardrails, as long as they don't slow down the spending.

Happy Thursday. In the same 48 hours, more than 1,100 AI industry employees — including the actual leadership of Anthropic and OpenAI — asked the US government to help build a coordinated slowdown mechanism for AI, while Meta and BlackRock signed a $14 billion check for more AI data centers and Moonshot AI's valuation nearly tripled to $35 billion. Nobody in this industry can agree on whether AI is moving too fast or not fast enough. Today's issue has receipts for both arguments.

Moonshot's Open-Source Flex Just Turned Into a $35 Billion Paycheck

China's Moonshot AI closed a $3.5 billion funding round at a $35 billion valuation, blowing past the $1–2 billion it originally set out to raise, per Bloomberg. The round was led by China's National Artificial Intelligence Industry Investment Fund — the same state vehicle that backs DeepSeek — and comes just two weeks after Moonshot open-sourced Kimi K3, a 2.8-trillion-parameter model that briefly became the largest open-weight system anyone can download for free. Investors are effectively betting that giving away your best model doesn't hurt your business — it advertises it.

Bottom line: open-sourcing your flagship model used to look like giving up your competitive edge; Moonshot just turned it into the pitch deck for a $35 billion valuation.

The People Building AI Just Asked Someone to Please Slow It Down

More than 1,100 employees at OpenAI, Anthropic, Google DeepMind, and Meta signed a letter circulated July 28 asking the US government to help build the technical and governance infrastructure for a verifiable, coordinated AI slowdown — not an immediate pause, but the tools to hit the brakes if AI capability ever outruns humans' ability to oversee it. Signatories include Anthropic CEO Dario Amodei and OpenAI chief scientist Jakub Pachocki. Both companies have since formally endorsed the letter on their own behalf, according to the Washington Post — a rare case of the people building the technology publicly asking for outside limits on themselves.

Bottom line: when the labs' own leadership signs a letter asking to be reined in, it's no longer activist theater — it's an admission that self-regulation alone isn't going to cut it.

Meta Just Wrote a $14 Billion Check to BlackRock for More AI Real Estate

Meta and BlackRock announced a strategic joint venture to build a 1-gigawatt AI data center campus in El Paso, Texas, valued at roughly $14 billion, with BlackRock-managed funds owning 80% and Meta retaining 20%. The first capacity is slated to come online in 2028. It's the latest sign that even Meta — which just raised its own 2026 capex guidance to as much as $145 billion — would rather share the bill with an outside financier than carry the entire AI buildout on its own balance sheet.

Bottom line: when a company with $90B+ in cash starts structuring its AI infrastructure like a real estate developer instead of just paying for it outright, that tells you how big these numbers have actually gotten.

Microsoft's AI Bet Paid Off. Meta's AI Bill Came Due.

Microsoft beat estimates across the board in its fiscal Q4 earnings, posting $90 billion in revenue as Azure growth hit 43% and pushed the cloud unit's annual revenue past $100 billion for the first time. A $3.2 billion mark-up on its Anthropic stake alone added 33 cents to earnings per share. Meta told a different story: revenue rose 28% to $60.8 billion, but net income fell 14% to $15.85 billion, partly on a $2.4 billion legal charge, and the company raised its 2026 capex guidance to as much as $145 billion — up from just $72.2 billion in 2025.

Bottom line: both companies are spending like the AI boom is permanent, but only one of them is currently getting paid for it.
Quick Hits
  • 🖥️ Nvidia shares slid about 2.3% Tuesday as investors weighed AI-capex sustainability against macro jitters — even as the chipmaker sits on $48.5B in quarterly free cash flow.
  • 🏭 AMD locked up more than 500 MW (with an option on up to 2.5 GW) of Core Scientific's US data center capacity through 2027, sending Core Scientific shares higher on the multi-gigawatt AI-compute bet.
  • 🔬 OpenAI opened ChatGPT for Academic Researchers, giving up to 100,000 scientists at qualifying universities free frontier-model access as part of a $250M+ research-support commitment through 2027.
  • 🕵️ AI-detection startup Pangram raised $9M and shipped Pangram 4, a text-and-image detector it claims spots AI-generated (and "humanized") content with over 99% accuracy.
  • 📱 Google confirmed Pixel 11 prices are rising as much as $100, blaming a sixfold jump in RAM costs as AI data centers absorb the world's memory-chip supply.
The Big Picture

Zoom out and the AI industry is running two completely different playbooks at once. One says the technology is advancing so fast that the people building it are asking governments for a verifiable off-switch — a genuinely unusual move for an industry that usually lobbies against oversight, not for it. The other says pour in more money, faster: Moonshot's valuation nearly tripled on the strength of a model it gave away for free, Meta and BlackRock structured a $14 billion data center deal like a real-estate play, and Microsoft's earnings beat was juiced by a stock gain on a rival lab. Both playbooks are being run by the same handful of companies, often in the same week. That's either a healthy tension between ambition and caution, or a sign that "please slow us down" is easier to sign than to actually mean.

One More Thing

Buried in Microsoft's earnings: its stake in Anthropic — a company it doesn't fully control and whose chatbot competes directly with Copilot — generated a $3.2 billion gain this quarter alone. Its stake in OpenAI, the company Microsoft has poured tens of billions into and built its entire AI strategy around, got marked down about $600 million over the same three months. Sometimes your side bet outperforms your main relationship.
