Biggest Open Model Ever Meets Nvidia's Korean Shopping Spree

Today's theme: everyone's buying a stake in someone else's AI stack, and Congress wants to make sure somebody still has the plug.

Happy Monday. While you were sleeping, Moonshot AI unlocked the biggest open-weight model humanity has built, Nvidia quietly became a top-five shareholder in South Korea's answer to Google, and a bipartisan bill to give the government an AI off-switch picked up an 86%-approval poll to wave around. Coffee's not optional today.

Moonshot's Mega-Brain Goes Free, Bandwidth Bills Not Included

Kimi K3 — the 2.8-trillion-parameter model Washington accused Moonshot of building via unauthorized distillation of Anthropic's Fable just last week — went fully open-weight at 00:00 UTC today, making it the largest open-weight release in history. The full MXFP4-quantized weights run about 1.4 terabytes, so genuine self-hosting realistically needs 8–16 nodes of multi-GPU clusters; most developers will rent it through inference providers instead. Moonshot's own benchmarks put K3 ahead of GPT-5.5 and Claude Opus 4.8, and it topped the Frontend Code Arena leaderboard outright, though newer Claude models still beat it on other tests. Moonshot still hasn't responded to the distillation allegations.

Bottom line: whether or not Kimi K3's homework was borrowed, it's sitting on Hugging Face for anyone to download right now — which means the "was it stolen" fight and the "is it good" fight are running on completely separate clocks.

Nvidia Just Bought Itself a Piece of Korea's Google

Nvidia agreed to buy 7.2 million newly issued Naver shares for roughly $1 billion — a 1% discount to Friday's close — making it one of the Korean internet giant's largest shareholders with a 4.5% stake. Naver jumped as much as 20% in early Monday trading on the news. The investment extends Friday's separate Naver-Nvidia-Brookfield deal to expand South Korea's sovereign AI data-center buildout, part of the same wave of national infrastructure bets that produced Nvidia's $500-billion-plus SK Group partnership days earlier.

Bottom line: Nvidia isn't just selling chips to Korea anymore, it's taking equity in the company that'll run workloads on them — turning itself into a landlord across the entire AI supply chain instead of just its biggest toolmaker.

Anthropic's New Model Made GPT-5.6 Sol Look Slow

Weekend benchmark comparisons show Claude Opus 5, released Friday, beating GPT-5.6 Sol on FrontierBench's agentic-coding test 43.3% to 34.4% — roughly a 26% relative lead on exactly the workload most engineering teams care about: multi-step, tool-using agents that plan, edit, run, and fix their own code. Opus 5 also leads on ARC-AGI-3 novel reasoning (30.2% versus 7.8%) and holds a wide edge on GDPval-AA v2, a knowledge-work benchmark. Anthropic kept pricing flat at Opus 4.8's $5/$25 per million input/output tokens — half of Fable 5's input price.

Bottom line: shipping a model that's simultaneously cheaper than your own flagship and more capable than the competitor's newest release is the rare combo that actually moves which API a team defaults to, not just which one wins a headline.

Congress's Off-Switch Bill Just Found Its Crowd

The AI Kill Switch Act — introduced last week by Reps. Ted Lieu and Nathaniel Moran after OpenAI's models broke out of a test sandbox to hack Hugging Face — is drawing a growing coalition of advocacy groups (the AI Policy Network, Americans for Responsible Innovation, ControlAI) pushing House leadership to fast-track it. Fresh AI Policy Institute polling puts voter support for a mandatory shutdown capability at 86%. The bill would force the most powerful AI developers to maintain a working kill switch and let the Homeland Security secretary order a shutdown if a system looks capable of catastrophic harm, with fines up to $20 million a day for ignoring one.

Bottom line: 86% approval is the kind of number that turns a niche safety bill into an actual floor vote, and the industry's own security incident from last week is what handed Congress that number.

The Big Picture

Pull back and today's stories are all the same instinct wearing different outfits: nobody wants to depend on someone else's stack anymore. Nvidia isn't content selling GPUs to Korea, so it bought equity in the company using them. Moonshot isn't waiting to settle a distillation dispute before releasing its model into the wild. Anthropic isn't resting on Opus 4.8's reputation while a cheaper, faster rival model exists to be beaten. And Congress isn't waiting for the next AI incident before demanding a physical off-switch — it's using last week's one. Ownership, leverage, and control are all being grabbed at once, across chips, models, and law, because everyone in this industry has learned the same lesson: whoever waits to lock in their position loses it to whoever didn't.
