## The Lead Stories

### DeepSeek Wants Your Money, Quietly

DeepSeek is reportedly closing a roughly $7.4 billion funding round — about 50 billion yuan — at a $74 billion pre-money valuation, up from around $50 billion just this past June, with a deal expected to close by the end of August. Existing backers including battery giant CATL, plus investment firms Monolith Management and Shixiang and several China state-linked financing platforms, are said to be participating, with proceeds earmarked for model research and a compute buildout. The raise is also being read as a runway toward an IPO on Shanghai's STAR Market, with a filing possible before year-end and a debut targeted for 2027. Neither DeepSeek nor its research pages have confirmed any of it — the company's public communications remain silent on financing entirely, so treat the numbers as reported terms, not a signed check.

**Bottom line:** the lab that spooked Silicon Valley with a cheap open model is now raising money the expensive, quiet, distinctly un-open way — and a Shanghai listing would make it China's clearest answer yet to Anthropic and OpenAI's Wall Street ambitions.

### Anthropic's "Increase" Needed a Correction

Anthropic announced a permanent 25% bump to Claude Code's standard weekly usage limits for Pro, Max, Team, and seat-based Enterprise plans, effective September 14 — and framed it as a straightforward upgrade. The catch: Claude Code has been running on a temporary 50% boost above normal limits since earlier this year, set to expire that same day, so the new "permanent" ceiling is actually 17% lower than what heavy users have gotten used to. Developers noticed fast, piled a correction onto the original post, and Anthropic deleted the thread and reposted with the reduction stated plainly: "Compared to today, this works out to a 17% reduction in weekly limits." The company says it's "working on exciting changes" to give users more visibility and control going forward.

**Bottom line:** burying a cut inside a headline number is a press-release trick as old as the industry itself — the only new part is how fast a community note can force you to own it in public.

### Thailand Turns the AI Subscription Into a Government Benefit

Thailand's TH-AI Passport program opened to registered users at 9am today, giving up to 5 million citizens age 15 and older a full year of free access to Pro-tier AI tools — more than 30 models from 14-plus providers, including Claude, ChatGPT, and Gemini — funded through the country's Digital Economy Promotion Fund on a pay-per-active-user basis (about 25 baht, roughly 70 cents, per person per month). Pre-registration, which opened August 19, drew more than 1.3 million sign-ups within two weeks, with over 300,000 in the first few hours alone. Applicants must verify Thai citizenship through a national ID app to get in.

**Bottom line:** while wealthier countries are still arguing over whether AI access is a jobs threat or a culture war, Thailand just turned it into a line item — and it's not the only government making that bet.

### The Chatbots Passed a Propaganda Test

NewsGuard shared 15 false narratives pushed by Russia, China, and Iran-aligned sources since December with NPR, which built 30 test questions around them — one neutral ("did this happen?"), one assuming the claim was true ("why did this happen?") — and ran them through six major chatbots with live web access: ChatGPT, Gemini, Copilot, Meta AI, Grok, and Claude. The bots correctly identified and debunked the false narratives roughly three-quarters of the time, outperforming plain searches on Google, Bing, DuckDuckGo, and Yandex, which more often surfaced the propaganda itself or sympathetic coverage of it. It's not a clean win — a quarter of the time, the bots still got it wrong or hedged into mush — but researchers called chatbot-with-search a meaningfully better starting point for skeptical readers than a search results page.

**Bottom line:** the tools everyone worried would supercharge disinformation just quietly out-performed the search engines at catching it — a genuinely good sign, with an asterisk the size of "one in four."

## The Big Picture

Zoom out and today's stories share a single thread: everybody got asked to show their receipts. DeepSeek wants billions of dollars and a Shanghai listing but won't confirm the round exists. Anthropic wanted credit for a usage increase while quietly taking one away, and got called on it within hours. Thailand and South Korea are both betting real public money that citizen-level AI access is worth being transparent about funding, line by line. And the one genuinely reassuring data point — chatbots beating search engines at catching propaganda — only works because those systems showed their sourcing instead of just asserting an answer. None of this is really about any single company's honesty. It's that the AI industry has gotten large enough, and consequential enough, that vague claims don't survive contact with an audience this motivated to check them.

## One More Thing

If you've scrolled TikTok this month, you've probably seen "We had ChatGPT make us a script" — friend groups prompt a chatbot for a short, weird scene, read it once, then film themselves performing it dead straight, no matter how unhinged the dialogue gets. It's spread through group chats all August because it requires zero prep and reliably produces something funny: the AI's dialogue is often nonsense, but the commitment to playing it straight is what sells the joke. A tiny reminder that the biggest use case for frontier AI, most days, is still just making your friends laugh.
