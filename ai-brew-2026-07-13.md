## The Lead Stories

### Alibaba and Anthropic Are Not Friends Anymore

Alibaba banned employees from using Anthropic's Claude Code starting July 10, telling staff to switch to its in-house tool Qoder instead, after security researchers claimed to find hidden code that detected whether users were in China or affiliated with Chinese AI labs and quietly altered its behavior for them. The ban lands weeks into an already ugly fight: Anthropic told the U.S. Senate Banking Committee in June that operators linked to Alibaba's Qwen lab ran roughly 25,000 fraudulent accounts to generate 28.8 million exchanges with Claude, calling it an industrial-scale attempt to copy its coding and reasoning abilities. Anthropic says the detection code was meant to curb account resale and model theft, not spy on anyone, and that it'll be stripped out in the next release. Alibaba hasn't addressed the fraud allegations directly.

**Bottom line:** Model "distillation" — training a cheaper model by hammering a rival's API — has quietly become the AI industry's insider-trading scandal, and this is the first fight over it to spill into full public view.

### Meta Doubts Its Own AI Bet, Then Doubles It

At a July 2 town hall, Mark Zuckerberg told employees that Meta's AI agent push "hasn't really accelerated" as expected and that its recent reorganization "hasn't come to fruition yet" — comments that knocked the stock down 5% that day. Meta's answer wasn't to slow down: Reuters reported the company is putting its custom "Iris" chip into production in September, signed long-term supply deals with Samsung for memory and Sandisk for flash storage, and is aiming to double its data center computing capacity to 14 gigawatts by 2027, backed by up to $145 billion in capital spending this year. Meta is also reportedly moving to lease out spare AI capacity as a new cloud business — a shift that sent competitor CoreWeave's stock down 14% on the news.

**Bottom line:** Zuckerberg just told his own staff the AI push is behind schedule, and then bet another $145 billion that it'll pay off anyway — a tell that in this industry, momentum matters more than proof.

### A Chipmaker's Valuation Triples in Five Months

SambaNova closed the first tranche of a $1 billion Series F at an $11 billion valuation, led by General Atlantic with BlackRock, Intel Capital, the Qatar Investment Authority, T. Rowe Price, and Capital Group all piling in — up from roughly $5 billion just five months ago. The bigger validation: JPMorgan Chase picked SambaNova as an inference-infrastructure partner, running its SN40L and SN50 chips for secure, on-premises AI inference inside the bank. It's a rare case of a Nvidia alternative landing a marquee enterprise customer instead of just another funding headline.

**Bottom line:** Every dollar chasing custom AI silicon is a dollar betting Nvidia's near-monopoly cracks eventually — and a bank trusting its inference workloads to SambaNova is more convincing than any pitch deck.

### College Kids Are Using AI. Their Classes Haven't Caught Up

Two of the largest higher-ed AI surveys ever conducted — a Lumina Foundation-Gallup study of nearly 4,000 U.S. students and a Digital Education Council survey of over 45,000 students and faculty across 35 countries — both landed this month with the same warning: adoption is outrunning guidance. Just 28% of students think most of their assessments actually reflect the skills they'll need in an AI-enabled workplace. Meanwhile 58% of U.S. and Canadian faculty said they're not worried their curriculum will be outdated by graduation — even though only 19% of their students feel the same.

**Bottom line:** Students are already using AI daily; their professors mostly aren't updating what "passing" looks like — a gap that's going to show up on résumés before it shows up on syllabi.

## The Big Picture

Today's stories all point at the same tension: the AI industry is simultaneously accelerating and getting caught doing sketchy things to keep up. Alibaba and Anthropic are fighting over who's stealing whose model weights because building a frontier model from scratch is brutally expensive — copying one is cheaper, so someone's always tempted to try. Meta's publicly hedging on whether its AI reorg is working while still spending $145 billion, because in a capital-intensive arms race, admitting doubt out loud is less risky than falling behind. SambaNova's valuation tripling and JPMorgan trusting it with real workloads shows investors and enterprises alike are actively hunting for alternatives to Nvidia's grip. And the college data is the quiet warning underneath it all: an entire generation is being trained by an education system that hasn't figured out what "trained" should even mean anymore. Move fast, worry later — it's the whole industry's operating system right now.
