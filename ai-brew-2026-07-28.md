## Editor's Open

Happy Tuesday. Nvidia spent Monday explaining why guaranteeing $600 billion of its own customer's bills isn't circular financing, Jensen Huang spent it explaining why the chip industry needs to get ten times bigger, and somewhere in the middle of all that a Chinese ad network quietly ran 7,600 face-swap ads through Meta's back door for months. Big week for "trust me."

## The Lead Stories

### Nvidia's Playing Banker, Landlord, and Chip Dealer All at Once

The Wall Street Journal reports Nvidia is in talks to backstop up to $600 billion of OpenAI-related financing: a $250 billion guarantee on the lease for a 10-gigawatt Ohio data center SoftBank's SB Energy is building on a former uranium-enrichment site, plus up to $350 billion financing OpenAI's actual chip purchases. The guarantee would help non-investment-grade OpenAI get construction lenders comfortable with the deal. Nvidia's own SEC filings cap its total lease-guarantee exposure at $3.5 billion — this single commitment would run roughly 71 times that. Short sellers Michael Burry and Jim Chanos piled on; Chanos noted Nvidia is essentially financing two-thirds of the cost of its own chip sales. Neither company has confirmed the reporting, and terms aren't final.

**Bottom line:** when your biggest customer needs you to guarantee its loan before it can buy your product, "insatiable demand" starts sounding a lot like demand you're subsidizing yourself.

### Meta's Ad Screeners Missed 7,600 Reasons to Worry

Instagram and Facebook served roughly 7,600 ads for AI "nudify" apps — tools that paste women's faces onto explicit images and videos — over several months, all funneled through a single Beijing-based ad partner called GatherOne, according to the nonprofit Tech Transparency Project and reported by Bloomberg. The ads violated Meta's own advertising policies, and one flagged app has reportedly hosted material some users identified as child sexual abuse material. GatherOne has since suspended new ad accounts tied to nudify and face-swap apps and says it's overhauling compliance; Meta hasn't detailed how the ads slipped past automated review for so long.

**Bottom line:** the same generative AI letting Meta automate everything from ad screening to your calendar is also what's making the bad actors on the other side of that automation harder to catch.

### Jensen Huang Wants 10x More Chips for 100 Billion New "Customers"

In a Bloomberg interview, Nvidia CEO Jensen Huang argued the semiconductor industry needs to grow roughly tenfold over the next decade — not to serve more people, but more agents. Today's roughly 1 billion human computing users, he said, will be joined by something like 100 billion AI agents and billions of robots, all needing their own compute. Huang pointed to Nvidia's physical-AI business, which he said has already hit a $10 billion run rate with a path to $100 billion, and leaned on South Korea as a proof case, citing Nvidia's $500 billion-plus SK Group partnership and this week's $1 billion Naver stake as templates.

**Bottom line:** Huang's math only works if 100 billion agents actually get built and put to work — which makes this forecast either the most useful roadmap in tech or the most expensive hypothesis ever pitched to investors.

### Earnings Week Is About to Ask AI Spending to Show Its Work

SK Hynix reports earnings today, the opening bell in a week testing whether Big Tech's AI spending is paying off. Microsoft and Meta report Wednesday, Apple and Amazon follow Thursday, all of it landing after Alphabet's capex guidance last week rattled a market already jumpy about AI-infrastructure costs — Nvidia shares slid toward $196 Monday, off as much as 6% from Friday's high. SK Hynix, which makes the high-bandwidth memory chips AI accelerators depend on, has redirected significant capacity to HBM, and its CEO warns the memory crunch could persist past 2030. Analysts expect Microsoft's capex alone to climb toward $119 billion next fiscal year, up 22% year-over-year.

**Bottom line:** investors have spent two years rewarding AI capex on faith; this week is when four of the biggest spenders have to show whether that faith is turning into cash flow.

## The Big Picture

Zoom out and every story today is really about trust and its absence. Nvidia wants investors to trust that a $600 billion IOU to its own customer isn't demand it invented for itself. Huang wants markets to trust a forecast built on 100 billion agents that don't exist yet. Meta's ad system just proved it can't be trusted to catch 7,600 policy violations from a single partner before they ran for months. And China's answer to all of it is to trust no one — not TSMC, not foreign buyers of its models, not outsiders eyeing its own tech firms. The AI industry is scaling faster than anyone's ability to verify what's actually happening inside it, and today was a pretty good preview of what that gap looks like in practice.
