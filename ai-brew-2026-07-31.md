# The AI Brew • July 31, 2026 • The AI Trade Gets Its First Report Card

## The Snapshot

- **$480B** — Microsoft's one-day market-cap gain Thursday, its best single-day pop since 2008, after Copilot seats nearly doubled quarter over quarter
- **91%** — the drop in Meta's quarterly free cash flow, to just $784M, as AI capex ate almost all its operating cash
- **$1.65B** — what Nscale is paying for Anyscale, the commercial steward of the open-source Ray framework
- **€30B** — total investment the EU is chasing with its new seven-site "AI Gigafactory" tender, opened for bids Thursday
- **$45B → forced sale** — peak size of Leopold Aschenbrenner's AI-focused hedge fund before Citadel had to step in and buy out its public stock bets
- *Today's theme: the AI trade just got its first real report card, and not everyone made the honor roll.*

## Editor's Open

Happy Friday. Thursday was earnings day for two of AI's biggest spenders, and Wall Street handed out very different grades: Microsoft popped nearly $480 billion in market value — its best day since 2008 — while Meta got hit with a 9% slide as free cash flow all but evaporated. Same AI boom, same enormous capex bills, opposite verdicts. Today's issue is really about one question: who's actually getting paid for all this spending, and who's just still writing checks?

## The Lead Stories

### Same AI Boom, Two Very Different Report Cards

Microsoft and Meta reported earnings within a day of each other this week, and investors could not have reacted more differently. Microsoft beat on every line — $90 billion in revenue, Azure growth accelerating to 43%, and Microsoft 365 Copilot seats nearly tripling to 30 million from 20 million in a single quarter — sending shares up as much as 17% Thursday, Microsoft's best day since 2008 and roughly $480 billion in added market value. Meta beat on revenue too, up 28% to $60.8 billion, but free cash flow collapsed 91% to just $784 million as $31 billion in quarterly AI capex ate nearly all its operating cash. Meta shares fell about 9%, extending a losing streak, on guidance that missed expectations.

**Bottom line:** investors aren't done believing in AI — they're just done paying for spending they can't see turning into revenue yet, and Microsoft's Copilot numbers are the first real proof that turn is possible.

### The Guy Who Warned Everyone About AGI Just Got a Margin Call

Leopold Aschenbrenner — the former OpenAI researcher whose 2024 essay "Situational Awareness" became required reading in AI circles — built a hedge fund, Situational Awareness LP, that reportedly returned 439% in the first half of 2026 and swelled to as much as $45 billion by early July. Then the trade turned: AI-infrastructure holdings like SK Hynix slid while a short bet against software companies including Adobe moved sharply against him, according to Bloomberg and CNBC. Facing margin pressure, the fund is unwinding its public positions, with Citadel stepping in to buy the bulk of them, while prime brokers including Goldman Sachs and JPMorgan help manage the wind-down.

**Bottom line:** even the person who literally wrote the book on seeing AI coming couldn't out-time a trade that had gotten too crowded and too leveraged.

### Europe Opens the Checkbook for Seven AI Megaplants

The European Commission formally opened bidding Thursday for its "AI Gigafactory" program, one of the bloc's most ambitious tech-sovereignty bets yet. Seven planned sites, each housing at least 100,000 AI chips — roughly four times the scale of a typical EU data center today — will split €10 billion in public funding, with the total expected to reach €30 billion once private capital joins in. The facilities are meant to give startups, universities, and public agencies access to serious training and fine-tuning infrastructure, not just the hyperscalers who can already afford it. Applications close November 12, with awards expected by mid-2027 and construction starting the same year.

**Bottom line:** Europe still can't outspend the US or China on AI infrastructure, so it's betting on wide access instead — turning compute into a public utility rather than a hyperscaler perk.

### Nscale Just Bought Its Way Up the AI Stack

UK-based AI cloud provider Nscale signed a definitive agreement to acquire Anyscale — the commercial steward of Ray, the open-source framework that quietly powers distributed training and inference for much of the AI industry — in a deal Bloomberg pegs at roughly $1.65 billion. Anyscale's engineering team, its customers (including Coinbase, Runway, and Bedrock Robotics), and its 70%-growing revenue base move to Nscale, while Ray itself stays open source under continued governance commitments. The logic: Nscale brings the power, real estate, and GPU clusters; Anyscale brings the software that actually schedules workloads across them.

**Bottom line:** owning the chips is no longer enough of a moat — the next fight in AI infrastructure is over who controls the software that decides how those chips get used.

## Quick Hits

- 🤖 Google DeepMind unveiled Gemini Robotics 2, a model that lets humanoid robots coordinate full-body movement — walking, crouching, manipulating objects — instead of just controlling the upper body, a step toward genuinely general-purpose robots.
- 💾 Arm Holdings beat estimates as data-center royalties more than doubled year over year on AI chip demand, but shares still fell about 8% after the company flagged softer smartphone royalties ahead.
- 🔥 Bloom Energy posted its first-ever $1 billion revenue quarter, up 166% year over year, and raised full-year guidance as hyperscalers and AI labs scramble to lock down reliable power for data centers.
- ⚖️ The FTC's public comment period closes today on a proposed policy statement that would treat AI companies' undisclosed ideological "steering" of model outputs as a deceptive trade practice.
- 📞 Encore AI raised a $30M Series A for agents that mine top sales performers' calls to drive revenue rather than just deflect support tickets — several bank and insurer customers converted into investors after using the product.

## The Big Picture

Zoom out and Thursday looked like the AI trade finally growing up. For a couple of years, "we're spending billions on AI" was itself the story that moved stocks. Now investors are starting to ask for receipts: Microsoft got rewarded because it can point to 30 million paying Copilot seats — real evidence spending is turning into revenue. Meta got punished because its capex is still outrunning any monetization story it can tell, no matter how fast revenue grows. Leopold Aschenbrenner's hedge fund blowup is the same lesson in miniature — a brilliant read on where AI capability was headed didn't protect him from a trade that got too big, too fast, too leveraged. Meanwhile, the EU and Nscale are still writing checks for infrastructure on the bet that today's spending becomes tomorrow's Copilot moment. The AI boom isn't slowing down; it's just starting to get graded.
