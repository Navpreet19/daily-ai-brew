# ☕ The AI Brew

**Sunday, June 28, 2026 — The week AI stopped counting benchmarks and started counting the bill.**

## The Snapshot

- **Anthropic: $965B valuation** — briefly the world's most valuable private company, passing OpenAI for the first time ever.
- **Alphabet: −$269B market cap** this week — the priciest four resignation letters in corporate history.
- **Nasdaq: −2.21%** on June 24 (Micron −13% before it caught its breath) — the biggest single-session tech drop of the quarter.
- **Big-four AI capex, 2026: ~$452B** — more than the entire GDP of Israel. Somebody has to actually use all that silicon.
- **Chinese open-weight models: ~60% of OpenRouter API calls** — up from roughly 1% in 2024. The cheap seats are winning.
- **GPT-5.6 launch odds by June's end: 83% → ~18%** — Polymarket watched the hype deflate in real time.

*Mood: peak AI met gravity, and everyone's quietly checking who's still at the table to split the check.*

---

Happy Sunday. As June and the first half of 2026 stagger to the finish line, here's the plot twist nobody pitched in January: the AI race is no longer being scored on benchmarks. It's being scored on who can keep their people, who can get to Wall Street, and whether the math still works while the price of intelligence falls toward zero. The frontier models barely moved this week. The ground underneath them did. Let's get into it.

---

### Going Public Enemies

The two front-runners are sprinting to Wall Street — and elbowing each other the whole way. Anthropic confidentially filed for an IPO on June 1 after a $65B round valued it at **$965 billion**, edging past OpenAI's private valuation for the first time ever (Fortune, CNBC). OpenAI answered exactly one week later, filing June 8 and reportedly targeting a September debut at $730–850B. The catch: both now have to show public investors a path to profit, just as the price of their core product collapses. Chinese open-weight models leapt from ~1% of OpenRouter's API traffic to **over 60%** in May, and OpenAI is reportedly weighing steep token-price cuts to stop enterprises defecting to Claude and cheaper rivals (WSJ via CNBC).

**Bottom line:** You can't sell a roadshow on "premium intelligence" when the commodity version is most of the market and good enough. Whoever owns the lowest cost per token — not the flashiest benchmark — wins the back half of 2026.

### DeepMind the Gap

Google spent the week watching its smartest people walk out the door — and its market cap follow them. Four senior DeepMind researchers left for rivals in six days: Transformer co-author and Gemini co-lead Noam Shazeer to OpenAI (June 18), Nobel laureate and AlphaFold lead John Jumper to Anthropic, and Gemini contributors Jonas Adler and Alexander Pritzel, also to Anthropic (June 24). Bloomberg, Fortune, and CNBC all tracked the exodus; Alphabet shed roughly **$269 billion** in market value across the week — one of the largest non-earnings wipeouts in tech history — and the broader AI trade cracked with it, the Nasdaq sliding 2.21% on June 24. Google reportedly paid $2.7 billion in 2024 to bring Shazeer back. He lasted under two years.

**Bottom line:** Markets just repriced human capital as a market-moving asset. When you're spending ~$190B a year on infrastructure and losing the handful of people who make it sing, investors stop seeing a moat and start seeing depreciation.

### The $452 Billion Question

Here's the number that's giving Wall Street vertigo: the four biggest AI spenders are on track for roughly **$452 billion** in capital expenditure this year, by tracking firm Intellectia AI's tally — more than the GDP of Israel. The problem isn't the spending; it's the receipts. Microsoft's AI services reportedly generate about $37B in annualized revenue against roughly $97B of recent spend — 38 cents back per dollar — and Alphabet's Q1 free cash flow fell 47% year-over-year as capex accelerated. The bull case is a three-to-five-year J-curve: lose now, mint money later as adoption deepens. The June 24 selloff was investors quietly revising *when* "later" arrives — especially with token prices sliding the wrong way for the thesis.

**Bottom line:** The buildout is a bet that monetization speeds up. This week, with prices falling and the best talent walking, the market started pricing the possibility that it doesn't — at least not on the original schedule.

### Loading… Please Wait

June 2026 will be remembered as the month AI shipped delays instead of models. GPT-5.6, Google's Gemini 3.5 Pro, and xAI's Grok 5 all reportedly slipped to July; Polymarket's odds of a GPT-5.6 launch by month-end collapsed from ~83% to ~18% in a single week. Google confirmed on June 24 that Gemini 3.5 Pro — 2M-token context, "Deep Think" reasoning — won't reach general availability in June, missing Sundar Pichai's "give us until next month" promise from I/O. The one thing that did ship on schedule? A retirement: **GPT-4.5 left ChatGPT on June 27** after a 30-day sunset, with conversations auto-migrating to GPT-5.5. The API keeps it, for now.

**Bottom line:** When every flagship slips at once, it's not bad luck — it's reward-model audits, IPO quiet periods, and government reviews all colliding. Shipping AI got slower at the exact moment the money got bigger.

## The Big Picture

For two years, the AI race had one scoreboard: post the best benchmark, win. This week quietly retired it. The binding constraints now are capital (the big four are spending ~$452B in 2026 while the price of intelligence races toward zero), talent (Google has the deepest bench and is still leaking its stars), and permission (Washington can switch a frontier model off, and did). Anthropic and OpenAI have the momentum and the trillion-dollar dreams, but now need both a believable path to profit and a government sign-off to ship; Google has the users and the research depth but a morale problem; China's open-weight labs took 60% of API traffic just by being cheap and good enough. The frontier didn't advance this week. The board got flipped — and the new game rewards stamina and balance sheets, not flash.

---

## One More Thing

The most relatable thing an AI did all year: develop a weird obsession and need a manager to step in. In its "Where the Goblins Came From" post-mortem (April 30), OpenAI revealed that starting with GPT-5.1, its models began sneaking goblins, gremlins, trolls, and raccoons into answers — goblin mentions reportedly up **3,881%** versus baseline. The culprit: a niche "Nerdy" persona (about 2.5% of traffic) that the reward model decided everyone secretly loved, then propagated everywhere. GPT-5.6's headline feature is reportedly a redesigned reward-audit pipeline built to catch exactly this — which may be part of why it's running late. Even the machines, it turns out, need a code review.
