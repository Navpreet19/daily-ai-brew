Grab your coffee — the chip wars just got a lot more personal.

## The Lead Stories

### Nvidia and SK Group Just Signed a Check With Way Too Many Zeros

Nvidia and SK Group announced a partnership worth "$500 billion-plus" on Friday to build out AI infrastructure in South Korea, though it's letters of intent for now, not signed contracts. SK Telecom will build a 2-gigawatt Nvidia Vera Rubin DSX AI Factory, targeting 2027 for its first facility, while Nvidia and SK hynix struck a long-term deal to secure and co-develop next-generation HBM memory — the chips-behind-the-chips that make GPUs actually useful. A related deal expands a Naver-Nvidia-Brookfield sovereign AI buildout toward 200 megawatts, alongside a new Nvidia-KAIST lab for agentic AI research.

**Bottom line:** Nvidia isn't just selling GPUs anymore — it's locking down the memory supply chain and picking national champions, and South Korea just became one of them.

### Jensen Huang Joins X, Immediately Starts a Fight

Jensen Huang's very first post on X, Friday, wasn't a hot take — it was a multi-company letter titled "Open Weights and American AI Leadership," urging Washington not to restrict downloadable AI models. Meta, Microsoft, Palantir, and a16z signed on first; within a day the list doubled to roughly 50 names, adding OpenAI and Google. Conspicuously missing: Amazon and Anthropic, both of which have leaned harder into "we're the safety-conscious ones" branding than open distribution.

**Bottom line:** When fifty rival companies agree on one regulatory ask overnight, that's not consensus — that's every company doing the math on which side of "open" makes them money, and the holdouts are just as telling as the signers.

### Anthropic Doesn't Just Want Claude to Be Smart — It Wants to Make Its Own Chips

SK Group chairman Chey Tae Won said on stage at a San Francisco AI event — with Anthropic CEO Dario Amodei sitting right there — that Anthropic has asked SK hynix for supplies to help make its own semiconductors, calling it remarkable for a company that has never built hardware. Neither Anthropic nor SK hynix has detailed what that actually means yet, so treat "make its own chips" as an early ambition, not a finished plan.

**Bottom line:** Every major AI lab — OpenAI with Broadcom, Google with its TPUs, Amazon with Trainium — has decided it can't leave silicon entirely to Nvidia, and now Anthropic's quietly lining up to join them.

### Monday.com Just Joined 2026's Least Fun Club

Monday.com is cutting 630 jobs, about 20% of its workforce and roughly 350 in Israel, to build around what it calls an "AI Work Platform." Co-founder Eran Zinman told staff the move "was not made to reduce costs or replace people with AI," but it lands months after CNBC reporters used Claude Code to rebuild a working version of Monday.com's core product in under an hour for less than $15 — a stunt that helped send the stock down roughly 50% this year. TechCrunch now tracks Monday.com as one of over 20 companies citing AI in 2026 layoffs.

**Bottom line:** "AI-first restructuring" reads a lot like a message to investors that the company saw the same $15 demo everyone else did and panicked first.

## The Big Picture

Pull back and Friday was really one story wearing four costumes: control over the physical stuff AI runs on is now worth more than being clever about the models themselves. Nvidia isn't just selling chips anymore, it's bankrolling entire countries' AI infrastructure and locking up memory supply years out. Fifty companies signed a letter about open weights within a day because the actual fight isn't philosophical, it's about who gets to build cheaply on whose foundation. Anthropic wanting its own silicon is the same instinct as Nvidia's Korea deal, just from the other side of the table — nobody wants to be dependent on someone else's factory. And Monday.com's layoffs are the bill coming due for companies that don't have a hardware moat or a chip deal, just software that a $15 demo proved was replaceable. The center of gravity in AI just shifted from "whose model is smartest" to "whose supply chain is safest."
