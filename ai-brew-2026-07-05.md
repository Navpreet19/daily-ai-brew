### Editor's Open

Most of the US spent July 4th watching fireworks. The AI industry spent it filing court documents, cutting funding term sheets, and drafting an offer for the federal government. No holiday, no slowdown — just four stories that quietly redrew who owns, controls, and governs this technology. Let's get into it.

---

### Lead Stories

**Sam Altman's Ownership Society**

OpenAI is reportedly floating a proposal to hand the US government a 5% equity stake in the company, worth roughly $42.6 billion at its current $852 billion valuation, according to CNBC and other outlets. The idea, still early and unconfirmed, would fold into a bigger pitch: an Alaska Permanent Fund-style vehicle where leading AI labs contribute stakes and everyday Americans get a dividend. CEO Sam Altman has framed it as a way to make sure the public shares in AI's financial upside instead of just absorbing the job-market fallout. The proposal needs congressional buy-in, and it's unclear whether Anthropic, Google, or Meta would follow suit. It also lands weeks after OpenAI confidentially filed IPO paperwork with the SEC.

**Bottom line:** giving the government a cap-table seat is as much a political hedge as a policy idea — it's hard to regulate aggressively a company you partly own.

**China Trains a Frontier Model Without Asking Nvidia**

Meituan open-sourced LongCat-2.0, a 1.6-trillion-parameter model with a 1-million-token context window, and says the entire training run — pre-training through inference — happened on a 50,000-card cluster of domestic Ascend 910 chips, no US hardware involved, per SiliconANGLE and the South China Morning Post. It's reportedly already climbing the charts on OpenRouter as an agentic coding model. Whether it fully matches frontier US models on real-world tasks is still being tested independently, but the headline claim — trillion-parameter scale on homegrown silicon — is itself the milestone Beijing has been chasing since export controls tightened.

**Bottom line:** export controls were supposed to slow China's frontier progress. This is the clearest evidence yet that they mostly redirected it toward chip self-sufficiency instead.

**Dario Draws a Line the Pentagon Didn't Like**

Court documents unsealed July 2 revealed tense emails between Anthropic CEO Dario Amodei and Pentagon Under Secretary Emil Michael, part of an ongoing legal fight over Anthropic's refusal to let the Defense Department use Claude for fully autonomous weapons targeting or domestic mass surveillance. The emails show Michael telling Amodei the two sides were "very close" on a contract — the same day the Pentagon quietly finalized a supply-chain risk designation against Anthropic, before telling the company. When Amodei said the proposed contract language would "completely remove" Anthropic's redlines, Michael reportedly didn't deny it, calling the guardrails "just not workable." An appeals court has so far denied Anthropic a stay.

**Bottom line:** this is the first real test of whether an AI lab's safety commitments survive contact with a government customer that doesn't want them.

**Crusoe Triples Down on the Data Center Bet**

Crusoe — the company that pivoted from flared-gas Bitcoin mining to AI data centers — is reportedly in talks to raise $3 billion at a $30 billion valuation, per Bloomberg, up from roughly $10 billion last October. The round would fund expansion of its nearly 5 gigawatts under contract and 40 gigawatts in its pipeline, serving Meta, Microsoft, and Google workloads. Nothing's signed yet, and valuations in this round of the infrastructure land grab are still being negotiated.

**Bottom line:** the fastest way to triple your valuation in AI right now isn't a better model — it's owning the power and the buildings the models run on.

### The Big Picture

Four stories, one thread: the fight has moved on from whose model is smartest. China just showed you can hit trillion-parameter scale without a single Nvidia chip, which blunts the whole premise of export controls. OpenAI is offering Washington equity before Washington decides to regulate it harder. The Pentagon and Anthropic are litigating who gets final say over autonomous weapons. And the UN is convening its first global AI governance summit this week because no single capital can referee any of this alone. The model race isn't over — but the compute, capital, and rulebook race just became the main event, and it's being played by nations and treasuries as much as by labs.

### One More Thing

If OpenAI's Alaska-style public fund idea ever actually happens, every American would technically become a tiny shareholder in the company that also, on a bad day, tells you glue doesn't belong on pizza. Dividend checks from your own chatbot — never bet against 2026.
