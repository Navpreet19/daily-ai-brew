## Editor's Open

Happy Wednesday. Tuesday didn't hand you a single new AI startup — it handed you a bitcoin miner playing AI landlord, an aerospace giant cashing its drone unit in for a slice of someone else's "physical AI" platform, and an AI lab opening a virtual classroom. If there's a theme, it's that the AI boom has stopped building its own real estate and started renting other industries' — sometimes literally, sometimes just their balance sheets. Grab your coffee; today's about who's hosting whom.

## The Lead Stories

### A Bitcoin Miner Becomes AI's Landlord

Riot Platforms — until recently known mostly for mining bitcoin — signed a 20-year, $9.1 billion deal Tuesday to supply Anthropic with 191 megawatts of computing capacity from its Rockdale, Texas campus, enough to power roughly 143,000 homes at any given moment. The agreement runs through 2048 and could grow to $16.1 billion if Anthropic exercises two five-year renewal options. It's the clearest sign yet that crypto miners are converting their power-hungry real estate into AI infrastructure faster than expected — one estimate puts AI and HPC work at up to 70% of listed miners' revenue by year's end. Riot's stock jumped roughly 25% in after-hours trading on the news.

**Bottom line:** Anthropic didn't just find more compute — it found a landlord who already knows how to run a power-hungry building at scale, because that's literally what bitcoin mining is.

### OpenAI Pays Employees $7 Billion to Stay Private

OpenAI completed a $7 billion tender offer Monday, letting current and former employees cash out shares at the same $852 billion valuation the company hit in its March fundraising round. Unlike prior buybacks — including a $6.6 billion tender at a $500 billion valuation last October — OpenAI funded this one itself rather than routing outside investor cash through the deal. The flat valuation, paired with a confidential SEC filing back in June to prepare for a possible IPO, reads less like momentum toward a public listing and more like OpenAI buying itself time: give employees liquidity now, skip the disclosure requirements and quarterly scrutiny a while longer.

**Bottom line:** when a company can write a $7 billion check to its own staff instead of asking Wall Street for one, "IPO soon" starts to sound like "IPO whenever we feel like it."

### Boeing Trades Its Drones for a Slice of "Physical AI"

Boeing agreed Tuesday to sell three subsidiaries — autonomous-flight pioneer Wisk Aero, defense-drone maker Insitu, and airspace-management platform SkyGrid — to electric-aircraft startup Archer Aviation, taking a roughly 20% equity stake in Archer plus warrants for up to $200 million more stock in return. The combined units bring nearly two million flight hours and, in Insitu's case, over $200 million in annual defense revenue across 35 countries; Archer plans to fuse them with its in-house aerospace model, ZEE, into what both companies are calling a "physical AI" platform for autonomy and defense. Archer shares jumped 20% on the news.

**Bottom line:** Boeing just admitted a scrappier AI-native company can do more with its own drone tech than Boeing can — and bet its future on owning a piece of that company instead.

### Anthropic Goes Back to School

Anthropic launched Claude for Teachers on Tuesday, a version of its assistant built to draft lesson plans, personalize materials to individual students, and align instruction with academic standards from all 50 states — joining OpenAI and Google, which have already rolled out their own classroom-specific tools this year. The pitch targets time-starved teachers rather than students doing homework, with Anthropic emphasizing data guardrails and standards-alignment over raw capability. It's a low-margin, high-trust market none of the frontier labs can afford to cede, given how much of the next decade's AI habits get set in classrooms.

**Bottom line:** the AI labs have stopped fighting only over which model is smartest and started fighting over who gets to teach your kid how to use one.

## Quick Hits

- 🔏 Anthropic said it'll start embedding invisible, machine-readable watermarks in text from new Claude models — worldwide, not just in Europe — to comply with the EU AI Act's transparency rules.
- 🤖 Unitree's Shanghai IPO closed 5,526x oversubscribed by retail investors, one of the biggest order books in Chinese market history, ahead of a trading debut expected around August 17-21.
- 🦙 Meta CEO Mark Zuckerberg doubled down on open-source AI, teasing a free release of the beefier Muse Spark 1.2 model and publishing a manifesto calling for both wider AI access and more government oversight.
- 📡 The FCC is reportedly drafting a ban on new Chinese-made optical transceivers in US data centers — components that carry over 60% of the high-bandwidth market today, meaning the ban could squeeze the very supply chain it's meant to protect.
- 📈 Riot Platforms shares surged as much as 25% Tuesday, with Bernstein raising its price target to $35 on the strength of the new Anthropic lease.

## The Big Picture

Zoom out and Tuesday's real story wasn't any single deal — it was what all four had in common: nobody built anything from scratch. Anthropic rented compute from a bitcoin miner instead of building its own data center from the ground up. OpenAI recycled its own balance sheet instead of tapping public markets. Boeing folded its drone business into a smaller, AI-native rival instead of competing with it. And the classroom AI race is really just labs bolting existing chatbots onto an existing curriculum. That's not a slowdown — it's a maturing industry realizing the fastest way to scale AI is to colonize infrastructure, capital, and institutions that already exist, rather than build new ones. The AI boom isn't inventing new bodies anymore. It's just finding new ones to wear.

## One More Thing

While everyone was signing deals, one number was easy to miss: Claude's monthly users climbed from about 60 million in December to roughly 245 million by May — a four-fold jump in five months — even as ChatGPT still commands more than half of all chatbot traffic worldwide. Behind every landlord deal and tender offer, it turns out actual humans are just... using the stuff.
