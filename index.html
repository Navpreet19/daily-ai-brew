THURSDAY, AUGUST 20, 2026

Everyone's Either Buying a Company or Publicly Denying It

The Snapshot
$7B+ — what Stripe is paying for AI model-router OpenRouter, with some reports pegging it above $8B, in the payments giant's biggest swing yet at owning a piece of the AI stack
$6.5B — Fractile's new valuation, up 6.5x in three months after Anthropic bet $250 million on chips the British startup hasn't even shipped yet
$75B — the all-time IPO record SpaceX set this year, and the number Anthropic just told bankers it's gunning to match or beat
$60B — SpaceX's all-stock purchase of coding startup Cursor, closed less than a week before it allegedly went shopping for a second AI company
$20B — the valuation Nvidia's discussing for a stake in Mercor, the AI data-labeling startup, roughly doubling its price tag from last October
Thursday's theme: nobody in AI bought just one company this month — they're buying, financing, or denying they tried to buy everything in reach.
Happy Thursday. Stripe closed the books on a $7 billion-plus AI acquisition, SpaceX reportedly went shopping for a second AI startup just days after finishing a $60 billion purchase of a first one, and Anthropic quietly told its bankers it wants to out-IPO the company that set the all-time IPO record a few months ago. Grab your coffee — today isn't about AI transforming the world so much as AI companies transforming their cap tables.

Stripe Buys Its Way Into Being an AI Company

Stripe confirmed Wednesday it's acquiring OpenRouter, the startup that lets businesses route requests across more than 400 AI models from 80-plus providers, in a deal reported at more than $7 billion — Axios and the New York Times put the number above $7.5 billion, though neither company disclosed exact terms. OpenRouter has scaled to 8 million users and counts Nvidia, Zoom, and Lovable among its customers, dynamically picking the best model for each request on price, speed, and reliability. CEO Patrick Collison framed it as a payments play as much as an AI one: "tokens are the central currency for companies building with AI," and Stripe wants to own the infrastructure for spending them wisely, not just collecting them.

Bottom line: a payments company just made its biggest-ever bet that managing AI spending will matter as much as building the models that spend it.

Anthropic Wants the IPO Record SpaceX Just Set

Anthropic told bankers it expects its upcoming IPO to match or exceed the $75 billion SpaceX raised in its own record-setting debut this year — a figure that climbed to $86.2 billion once SpaceX's overallotment option kicked in. The Claude maker, working with Morgan Stanley, Goldman Sachs, and JPMorgan, could file publicly as soon as the end of this month, building on the $65 billion annualized revenue run rate and roughly $2 trillion valuation talk it's floated all week. If Anthropic pulls it off, 2026 would become the highest annual U.S. IPO year on record — deals have already raised $160.6 billion so far, within reach of 2021's $195.2 billion peak.

Bottom line: chasing down a record that's only a few months old, set by a rocket company, tells you how fast the ceiling on "biggest ever" keeps moving this year.

Nvidia Builds China a Chip That Plays by Nvidia's Rules

Nvidia plans to start shipping small volumes of a new AI chip built specifically for Chinese customers by year-end, The Information reported — a version of its LPU line built on technology licensed from Groq, designed to work alongside its GPUs to speed up chatbot responses rather than train models outright. The chip is engineered to comply with existing U.S. export controls, and several Chinese customers have reportedly already placed orders. What's unclear is whether Beijing will actually let it in: China has spent recent weeks quietly discouraging firms from bringing even approved American chips onto the mainland, and its commerce ministry is separately weighing its own export curbs on AI models, training data, and chip designs.

Bottom line: Nvidia's cleared Washington's checklist for this chip — the harder approval it now needs is Beijing's, and that one isn't about export law at all.

The $250 Million Bet That Made Fractile's Valuation Explode

British AI-chip startup Fractile is in talks to raise roughly $600 million at a $6.5 billion valuation, led by Lightspeed Ventures and Redpoint, up more than sixfold from the roughly $1 billion valuation it held just three months ago. The jump followed Fractile striking an initial deal to sell Anthropic $250 million worth of inference chips — hardware that isn't expected to actually ship until 2027 — with room to expand the contract later. Founded in 2022 by Oxford roboticist Walter Goodwin, Fractile builds specialized systems aimed at making AI inference cheaper and faster than running it on Nvidia GPUs.

Bottom line: in this market, a single pre-revenue contract with the right buyer is worth more than most companies' entire balance sheets — the chips don't even have to exist yet.
Quick Hits
  • 🤖 Nvidia is discussing an investment in Mercor, the AI data-labeling startup, as part of a round that would value it at $20 billion — Nvidia is already one of Mercor's biggest customers, paying tens of millions last quarter for expert-curated training data.
  • 🔒 OpenAI previewed "Private Safety Processing," a system meant to flag AI misuse without exposing customer prompts to staff — a direct answer to Anthropic's own shift toward 30-day data retention on its most capable models.
  • 🚧 China's commerce ministry is reportedly weighing a rule barring TSMC from manufacturing chips based on designs from Chinese firms like Alibaba and ByteDance, part of a broader push to keep frontier AI hardware and models at home.
  • ⚙️ Coding-tool maker Warp opened a closed beta of "Factories," version-controlled pipelines that let fleets of AI coding agents move a ticket through spec, implementation, and review with human checkpoints along the way.
  • 📈 OpenAI CFO Sarah Friar told staff at an all-hands the company "will be a public company in 2027," or sooner if growth keeps accelerating the way it has this year.
The Big Picture

Zoom out and today's stories are all reaching for the same lever: buy your way into position rather than build your way there. Stripe didn't build a model router, it bought the best one on the market. Nvidia isn't just building its own China-compliant chip, it's also bankrolling a data-labeling startup and, separately, a scrappy UK challenger — hedging its bets across the entire stack instead of picking one horse. Even the deal that didn't happen fits the pattern: money is so abundant right now that acquisition, or at least the rumor of one, has become the fastest way to plant a flag. And Anthropic gunning for an IPO record that a rocket company set only months ago is really the same story at a bigger scale — in an industry moving this fast, "biggest ever" has stopped being a milestone and started being a moving target everyone expects to clear.

One More Thing

Buried under all the actual deals: SpaceX reportedly approached Cognition, maker of the AI coding agent Devin, about an acquisition — just five days after officially closing its $60 billion, all-stock purchase of coding startup Cursor. Cognition CEO Scott Wu wasn't having it, publicly denying the Bloomberg report and insisting the company "is not for sale" and that the two sides haven't even talked. Bloomberg's sources maintain otherwise, though both companies agree on one thing: they're now discussing letting Cognition run its AI agents on SpaceX's computers instead. Somewhere, a PR team is very carefully wordsmithing the difference between "we tried to buy them" and "we're just talking about servers."
