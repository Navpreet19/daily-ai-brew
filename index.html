AMD Bets Big on Anthropic, Wall Street Blinks on AI Capex

The Snapshot
$5B / 2 GW — AMD's new investment in Anthropic, which is committing to buy up to 2 gigawatts of MI450 chips starting in 2027
-$1.1B — Tesla's free cash flow this quarter, swinging negative as AI and robotics spending outran sales
82% — Google Cloud's revenue growth last quarter, a beat that still didn't stop Alphabet's stock sliding on capex jitters
$5.25M — the price of a single AMD Helios AI rack, unveiled this week with Microsoft as its first big buyer
88% — share of students worldwide now using AI in their coursework, per a new 35-country survey
Today's theme: money's still pouring into AI infrastructure by the gigawatt, but investors just started asking for the receipts.

Thursday, and AI's biggest spenders are doubling down at the exact moment Wall Street starts getting twitchy about the bill. AMD wrote Anthropic a $5 billion check hours after Alphabet and Tesla both beat estimates and got punished anyway for how much they're pouring into AI infrastructure. Beijing, meanwhile, is quietly discussing whether its own open AI models have gotten too valuable to keep handing out for free. Let's get into it.

AMD Writes Anthropic a $5 Billion Chip Order

AMD said it will invest up to $5 billion in Anthropic, with the AI lab committing to buy as much as 2 gigawatts of AMD's next-generation Instinct MI450 GPUs starting in the first half of 2027 — enough compute capacity to rival a small country's power grid. The deal landed the same week as AMD's Advancing AI conference, where the company also unveiled Zen 6 "Venice" server chips (the first x86 CPU built on TSMC's 2nm process) and its first public roadmap for 2027's Instinct MI500, which AMD claims will deliver up to 1,000x the AI performance of 2023's MI300X. For Anthropic, it's compute diversification insurance against relying entirely on Nvidia; for AMD, it's proof a top-tier lab is willing to pre-commit its roadmap to hardware that doesn't exist yet.

Bottom line: AMD didn't just sell Anthropic some chips — it bought itself a marquee reference customer for silicon it hasn't shipped.

Big Tech's Earnings Beat the Numbers, Missed the Vibe

Alphabet and Tesla kicked off Big Tech earnings Wednesday, and the pattern was identical: strong headline numbers, punished stocks. Google Cloud revenue jumped 82% year-over-year and Alphabet beat on both the top and bottom lines, but shares fell after-hours once executives raised capex guidance again — investors are increasingly nervous about how much of that spending buys durable advantage versus just keeping pace with rivals. Tesla posted $28.24 billion in revenue, up 26%, but adjusted profit of $0.33 a share missed the $0.50 estimate, and free cash flow swung to a $1.1 billion deficit as AI and robotics investment outran sales. Microsoft, Amazon, and Meta — all reporting in the coming days — dropped in early trading on the same worry.

Bottom line: The AI trade isn't broken, but the market just started grading it on results instead of ambition.

Beijing Might Build Its Own Export Wall

China's Ministry of Commerce is weighing new export controls on homegrown AI models and chips, the Financial Times reported, in a mirror image of the restrictions Washington has spent years imposing on Chinese access to advanced U.S. silicon. Regulators have reportedly consulted top domestic AI and chip firms — including Alibaba and ByteDance — about limiting how much training data and model weights can flow overseas, and whether foreign users should keep being able to download China's most capable open models at all. Nothing is finalized, and officials stress no timeline exists. But the shift in posture is notable: Beijing spent the last two years positioning cheap, open Chinese models as the industry's global alternative to Western labs — and is now weighing whether that same openness has become a liability.

Bottom line: When the country that built its AI brand on giving models away starts talking about locking the door, that tells you how valuable the models have gotten.

Microsoft Buys Mistral a Bigger Server Room

Microsoft and Mistral expanded their strategic partnership this week with a multibillion-dollar commitment to build out Mistral's European computing infrastructure using thousands of Nvidia Vera Rubin GPUs — no new equity stake involved. The deal gives Azure customers access to Mistral's France-based data centers and lets European enterprises and regulated industries run frontier AI without routing sensitive data through U.S.-controlled infrastructure, a growing ask as data-sovereignty rules tighten across the continent. Mistral is also adding its Medium 3.5 and OCR 4 models to Microsoft's Foundry platform, giving Azure developers more non-U.S. model options out of the box.

Bottom line: Microsoft is bankrolling Europe's AI independence — partly generosity, mostly a hedge against the day Brussels decides American clouds aren't the default anymore.

  • 💸 Amazon, Microsoft, and Meta all slid in early Wednesday trading ahead of their own earnings, investors bracing for the same AI-capex scrutiny that hit Alphabet and Tesla.
The Big Picture

Two things are true about AI spending right now, and today proved both. It's still accelerating — AMD just locked in a multi-year, multi-gigawatt customer in Anthropic, and Microsoft is bankrolling an entire European compute buildout for Mistral. And it's starting to get graded on results, not ambition — Alphabet and Tesla both beat estimates and got punished anyway, because "we're spending more on AI" stopped being reassuring and started being a question mark. Layer in Beijing quietly weighing whether its own open models have become too valuable to keep giving away, and the read on this cycle gets clearer: infrastructure is still being built at a wartime pace, but the capital funding it — public and private alike — is starting to ask when the payoff actually shows up.

One More Thing

Curious what AMD's new flagship costs? A single fully loaded Helios AI rack runs about $5.25 million — north of ten average U.S. homes, though this one draws over 130 kilowatts, packs dozens of GPUs, and unlike your house, gets more valuable the moment a hyperscaler plugs it in. Somewhere, a procurement officer is drafting the purchase order right now.
