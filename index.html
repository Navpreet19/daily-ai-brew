OpenAI cooks its own silicon — and brings the heat

Happy Friday. Summer's barely a week old, and instead of easing into vacation mode, the AI labs spent the week brawling over the two things no model runs without: silicon and the people who design it. Today is about the stuff underneath the chatbot — chips, gigawatts, and a talent market now poaching Nobel laureates. Let's dig in.

Bring Your Own Chip

OpenAI doesn't just want to write the recipe — it wants the stove. On Wednesday, it and Broadcom unveiled Jalapeño, OpenAI's first custom chip, built specifically for inference (the part where a finished model actually answers you, not the months of training that taught it how). OpenAI says the chip went from design to "tape-out" — finalized and shipped to the fab — in about nine months, which it claims is among the fastest sprints ever for silicon this size. Early tests show "substantially better performance per watt" than today's best, per the company — a not-so-subtle jab at Nvidia, whose shares slipped ~1.6% on the news while Broadcom rose ~1%. The plan: roll out on the order of 10 gigawatts of these accelerators with Microsoft over the coming years, starting late 2026.

Bottom line: Training gets the headlines, but inference is the part you pay for every single day. If OpenAI can shave the cost of answering you, it loosens Nvidia's grip on AI's most expensive line item — and every rival lab just got a reason to design its own.

Brain Drain at the 'Plex

Google keeps inventing the future, then watching it walk out the door. Per TechCrunch (citing Bloomberg), two researchers central to Gemini — Jonas Adler and Alexander Fritzel — are leaving for Anthropic. They're not alone: Noam Shazeer, a co-author of the 2017 Transformer paper that kicked off this whole era, is reportedly headed to OpenAI, and John Jumper, who shared the 2024 chemistry Nobel for AlphaFold, is leaving DeepMind for Anthropic. Google had paid up to keep some of these folks — including an effective ~$2.7B reacquisition of Shazeer's startup Character.AI. Didn't stick.

Bottom line: Models can be cloned in months; the people who dream up the next architecture can't. The scarcest asset in AI isn't GPUs — it's the few hundred minds who know what to build with them, and right now Anthropic and OpenAI are writing the bigger checks.

Chips Ahoy, Brussels

Washington is shopping for friends. On Thursday, Bloomberg reported the US has pitched the European Union on a formal AI partnership aimed at locking down semiconductor supply chains as competition with China sharpens — built on "trusted collaboration, economic security, innovation, and fair competition." The timing's delicate: Europe's own AI Act starts biting on August 2, when its rules for "high-risk" systems kick in, and the two sides haven't exactly seen eye to eye on regulation. Meanwhile, allies aren't waiting around — a wave of "sovereign AI" funds and national strategies has countries racing to own their compute rather than rent it.

Bottom line: Compute is the new oil, and 2026 is the year governments started acting like it. Expect more "trusted partner" alliances — and more nations quietly deciding they'd rather own the whole stack than lease it from Silicon Valley.

The Big Picture

Strip away the branding and this week told one story: AI's value is migrating from the model to everything around it. When a frontier model can be matched in months, the durable advantages become the boring, capital-heavy stuff — custom silicon, gigawatts of power, distribution, and the handful of researchers who know what to build next. That's why OpenAI is taping out its own chips, why Google is bleeding talent to richer rivals, and why governments are treating compute like a strategic reserve. With roughly $725B in hyperscaler capex flowing this year, the "AI race" is quietly becoming an industrial-policy race. Follow the money: it's pooling in infrastructure and expertise, not in another chatbot.

One More Thing

OpenAI named its first chip Jalapeño. No official word on why, though we like to picture a whiteboard, a late night, and someone insisting the next one be called Habanero. For scale: a jalapeño tops out around 8,000 Scoville units of heat; OpenAI's tops out around 10 gigawatts of the electrical kind — enough to power a small country, and decidedly worse on nachos. Stay cool out there. 🌶️
