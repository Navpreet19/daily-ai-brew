## Editor's Open

Happy Thursday. Tuesday and Wednesday together delivered a full news cycle: Gemini crossed a billion monthly users, Google shipped a phone chip a manufacturing generation ahead of anything else on the market, Anthropic started fielding hard investor questions ahead of a possible trillion-dollar IPO, and a coding startup wants to be worth 50% more than it was three months ago. Then, almost as a rebuttal, a security firm revealed a months-old open-source breach nobody fully understood until now, and Beijing reminded everyone that Chinese AI startups don't just get to change owners because Silicon Valley wants them to. Grab your coffee — today's about the gap between how fast AI is scaling and how fast everything underneath it is catching up.

## The Lead Stories

### Gemini Crosses a Billion Users, Then Google Doubles Down With Hardware

Google's Gemini app passed 1 billion monthly users Tuesday, CEO Sundar Pichai announced — Google's fastest-growing product ever, climbing from 400 million in May 2025 to 900 million this May, then adding another 100 million-plus in under a month. That count only covers people opening the standalone app; it excludes the separate billion-plus who encounter Gemini through AI Overviews in Search or through Gmail and Docs. A day later, Google doubled down at its "Made by Google" event, unveiling the Pixel 11 lineup built around Tensor G6 — the first smartphone chip to ship on a 2-nanometer process — with Gemini 3.6 Flash now running locally for tasks like call screening, no round-trip to Google's servers required. The Pixel 11 starts at $899; the redesigned Pixel Watch 5 ($399) notably ships without full Gemini support on day one.

**Bottom line:** Google spent Tuesday proving people already live inside Gemini, then spent Wednesday proving it can run in your pocket without needing the internet to do it.

### Anthropic Starts Its IPO Charm Offensive

Anthropic is meeting with prospective investors ahead of a planned Nasdaq debut this fall, the Wall Street Journal reported this week, pitching a $47 billion annualized revenue run rate, 130% projected revenue growth, and what would be the company's first operating profit — against a last private valuation of $965 billion that some bankers think could price above $1 trillion once shares actually trade. The meetings aren't going entirely smoothly: investors are reportedly pressing executives on cheap Chinese open models undercutting Anthropic's pricing, tightening Trump administration AI policy, and local resistance to new data centers. Anthropic's response, per the Journal, has mostly been to steer the conversation back to model quality rather than engage the China question head-on.

**Bottom line:** you don't get grilled about Chinese competition and White House politics unless investors already think you're about to be worth a trillion dollars.

### A Coding Agent Wants a 50% Valuation Bump in 90 Days

Cognition, maker of the AI coding agent Devin, is reportedly already in talks for a new funding round that could value the company north of $40 billion — up from the $26 billion it hit just three months ago in a $1 billion raise. The case for the jump: Devin's enterprise usage has grown 50% month-over-month for six straight months, and Cognition's annualized revenue run rate has roughly doubled since May, from $492 million toward $1 billion now. Customers reportedly include Citi, Mercedes-Benz, Goldman Sachs, and even the U.S. Army and Navy, mostly using Devin for unglamorous work like migrating legacy codebases rather than headline-grabbing greenfield builds. Nothing's signed yet, and reported valuations have a habit of shrinking before ink hits paper.

**Bottom line:** when a funding round shows up before the last one's champagne goes flat, the market isn't pricing what Devin does today — it's pricing whoever writes code for a living tomorrow.

### Beijing Sends a Chinese AI Startup Back Home

Manus, the agentic-AI startup Meta agreed to buy for roughly $2 billion last December, said this week it will resume operating as a fully independent company — the final step in unwinding a deal China's National Development and Reform Commission ordered killed back in April over foreign-investment concerns. Manus's original backers, led by Tencent (now its largest shareholder) alongside ZhenFund and HSG, already bought the company back from Meta for the same $2 billion. The independence move comes with a catch: to satisfy regional rules, Manus will delete certain user data generated since December 29, giving affected users until August 23 to back it up before service is restored two days later.

**Bottom line:** Beijing didn't just block a sale — it routed one of China's most promising AI agent startups straight into the hands of Tencent, a company Meta was trying to out-buy in the first place.

## Quick Hits

- 🛡️ Security firm CloudSEK said this week a five-month-old supply-chain breach of the widely used LiteLLM tool — traced to a hijacked security-scanner token — potentially exposed over 2,500 companies, including Nvidia, Samsung, Cisco, and Deloitte, and 434,000 CI/CD pipelines; the FBI warned in July the stolen credentials are still usable.
- 🤖 xAI released Grok 4.6 on Wednesday, matching OpenAI's GPT-5.6 Sol on a leading AI benchmark and trailing Anthropic's Fable 5 by a single point — barely a month after Grok 4.5 shipped.
- 🏭 TSMC's July revenue jumped 44.7% year-over-year to $14.5 billion, with its new 2-nanometer process — the same node inside Google's Tensor G6 — now doing the heavy lifting.
- ⚖️ The EU's AI Act entered a new enforcement phase this month, requiring visible labels on AI-generated deepfakes and chatbot disclosures; more than 180 companies have signed a voluntary compliance code, with fines up to €15 million or 3% of global revenue for stragglers.
- 🎓 New tracking data this week found 21% of companies have already frozen entry-level hiring because of AI, with another 15% saying they'll follow by year's end.

## The Big Picture

Zoom out and the throughline across today's stories is a widening gap between AI's front end and its back end. On the front end: Gemini just crossed a billion monthly users, Cognition's coding agent might be worth $40 billion, and Anthropic is measuring itself for a trillion-dollar valuation — genuine, fast, real adoption and real revenue. On the back end: a five-month-old breach in one open-source tool touched thousands of companies before anyone noticed, a Chinese startup got yanked between two governments over who's allowed to own it, and even the world's most valuable AI lab is fielding uncomfortable questions from the investors it needs most. None of that infrastructure — legal, financial, or technical — is keeping pace with how fast the front end is scaling. That's not a reason to bet against AI; it's a reason to bet the next twelve months are about hardening the plumbing, not just shipping more front door.

## One More Thing

Somewhere between chasing billion-user milestones and trillion-dollar IPOs, the AI industry also quietly shipped a watch that might notice you're in trouble before you do. Wear it like you mean it.
